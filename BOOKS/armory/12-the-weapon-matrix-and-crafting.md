# Chapter Twelve — The Weapon Matrix and Crafting

**Status: DRAFT, for review.** The first of the Armory's four remaining chapters. Two
systems in one chapter because they answer the same question from opposite ends: **what a
character has at the start, and how they get anything else.**

---

Eleven chapters of this book have catalogued what exists. **This one is about acquisition.**

A character does not begin with the catalogue. They begin with a specific, short list
determined by their class, one feat, and one profession — and everything after that comes
from a shop, a corpse, or a workbench that this project has decided is not a piece of
furniture.

---

# Part One — The Weapon Matrix

**⚠ The Player's Handbook is the authority on starting equipment; this is a summary of it.**
The table below exists so you can see every class's opening kit at once. **Where it and the
Player's Handbook disagree, the Player's Handbook is right.**

## Four columns, because two things modify a starting kit

Every class's weapons resolve four ways:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Default** | what the class starts with |
| **+ `Two-Weapon Fighting`** | the feat |
| **+ profession** | **`Hunter` upgrades melee. `Veteran` upgrades ranged.** Neither names a weapon of its own |
| **+ both** | the feat's *two* and the profession's *better*, together |

## ⚠ The melee option is a choice. The ranged one is not

**This is the asymmetry that explains the whole table:**

    MELEE   two one-handed weapons OR one double-bladed. Every class picks.
    RANGED  two one-handed blasters. No choice, because there is nothing
            to choose.

**It falls straight out of Chapter One's wield classes.** Class 3 — the double-bladed
family — is **entirely melee**; no ranged weapon in either game is wield 3. A rifle is
wield 5 and cannot pair at all. A pistol is wield 4 and pairs only with another one-hander.

> **So the ranged cell is locked by the source, not by design — there is no
> double-barrelled analogue to a Quarterstaff.**

| **Smuggler** | Blaster Pistol · Short Sword | two Blaster Pistols *or* two Short Swords *or* a Quarterstaff | **Hunter:** Long Sword · **Veteran:** Heavy Blaster | two Heavy Blasters *or* Long Sword + Short Sword *or* a Double-Bladed Sword |
| **Marksman** | Marksman Rifle · Hold Out Blaster | two Hold Out Blasters *(ranged, locked)* · **or** two Short Swords *or* a Quarterstaff | **Veteran:** Blaster Pistol | two Blaster Pistols |

| **Duelist** | Vibroblade | two Vibroblades *or* a Vibro Double-Blade | **Hunter:** Vibrosword | Vibrosword + Vibroblade *or* a Vibro Double-Blade |

**The Brawler's row is not an oversight.** A class whose weapon is its hands gains nothing
from a weapon feat or a weapon profession, and the table says so rather than inventing a
grant to fill the cell.

## The ceiling, and the one class allowed past it

**The feat-plus-profession line is capped at the `Double-Bladed Sword`** — `2d6`, 100
credits, `Balanced`, two attacks.

**The tier-1 melee ceiling is actually one rung higher**, and the ruling stops below it on
purpose:

    Double-Bladed Sword   2d6 · 100cr   — the cap
    Vibro Double-Blade    2d8 · 200cr   — reserved for the Duelist

**That keeps the Duelist at the top of the melee ladder**, which is the only class whose
whole identity is one blade.

### The Duelist's pairing, and why it is shaped that way

The `Hunter` grant is a **Vibrosword** (`1d12`) and the off hand keeps the **Vibroblade**
(`1d10`) — *which is `Balanced`, and the Vibrosword is not.*

> **Biggest weapon main hand, balanced weapon off hand.**

**That shape is already correct everywhere else in the table**; the Duelist was
simply brought into line with it.

### What the feat gives, and what the profession gives

**⚠ A mistake worth understanding, because it affects five rows.** It is tempting to read
*"two Heavy Blasters"* as something the feat alone provides. **It is not.**

    Feat only        two Blaster Pistols
    Profession only  one Heavy Blaster
    Both             two Heavy Blasters

**The feat gives *two*. The profession gives *better*.** Only together do they give two
better ones. The same correction applies to the Smuggler, Engineer, Treasure Hunter and
Marksman rows.

## The six Force classes

| Class | Default | + Two-Weapon Fighting |
|---|---|---|
| **Jedi Guardian** | Training Lightsaber — *blue* | Training Lightsaber + Training Short Lightsaber *or* a Training Double-Bladed Lightsaber |
| **Jedi Sentinel** | Training Lightsaber — *yellow* | as above |
| **Jedi Consular** | Training Lightsaber — *green* | as above |
| **Sith Warrior** | Training Lightsaber — *red* | as above |
| **Sith Assassin** | Training Lightsaber — *red* | as above |
| **Sith Inquisitor** | Training Lightsaber — *red* | as above |

**There is no profession column, and the reason is good:** a Force class's weapon upgrade
is **the real lightsaber**, and that is **a campaign event rather than a profession
grant.** Nobody is handed a Jedi's weapon at character creation because they
picked the right background.

**The `Initiate` profession grants a crystal or a saber upgrade** — it improves the blade
they have rather than replacing it, which is Chapter Four's crystal subsystem doing the
work.

**Chapter Four's Training Lightsaber is this table's weapon.** `1d8`, a step below the
Short Sword's cousin and well below the real thing. **It is what you learn on**, and every
Force class in the game starts with one.

## The droid arrays

**A droid may take only nine of the eighteen classes:**

    No Force class        all six — CLASS-ROSTER-01
    No Combat-rate class  Soldier · Marksman · Brawler

**Nine remain:** Scout, Smuggler, Bounty Hunter, Engineer, Machinist, Agent, Treasure
Hunter, Medic, Duelist.

**And every droid array is ranged**, because no chassis may use melee weapons:

| Class | Droid default | + Two-Weapon Fighting |
|---|---|---|
| **Scout** | Blaster Carbine — *the Short Sword is dropped* | two Blaster Pistols |
| **Smuggler** | Blaster Pistol | two Blaster Pistols |
| **Bounty Hunter** | Blaster Pistol — *the Stun Baton is dropped* | two Blaster Pistols |
| **Engineer** | Ion Blaster | two Ion Blasters |
| **Agent** | Hold Out Blaster | two Hold Out Blasters |
| **Treasure Hunter** | Blaster Pistol | two Blaster Pistols |
| **Medic** | Hold Out Blaster | two Hold Out Blasters |
| **Machinist** | Blaster Pistol — *its array weapon is a Long Sword* | two Blaster Pistols |
| **Duelist** | Blaster Pistol — *its array weapon is a Vibroblade* | two Blaster Pistols |

**Six classes drop a melee half; two had nothing but melee and substitute a pistol.**

**A Duelist droid still works**, and the wording already allowed it: `Dueling` reads *"a
single **blaster pistol**, melee weapon, or lightsaber."*

**Armour and hardware:**

| Chassis | Armour | Hardware |
|---|---|---|
| **Battle · Assassin** | Droid Light Plating Type 1 — 75cr | — |
| **Astromech · Remote** | none | **Shock Arm** — chassis hardware, see Chapter Ten |

**The Shock Arm occupies no weapon slot**, so pairing is unaffected — an Astromech with the
feat carries two pistols **and** its arm.

**A droid takes no profession.** There is no third or fourth column.

# Part Two — Crafting

## The whole rule, in one sentence

> **Crafting at a base during eight hours of uninterrupted work always succeeds if your
> skill reaches the DC. Crafting anywhere else is a roll.**

**Everything below is that sentence expanded. Nothing else is new mechanism.**

## Why there is no roll at a base

**This is not a crafting rule. It is the ordinary skill rules applied.** You may take 10 on
a skill check where there is no pressure and no distraction — and **eight uninterrupted
hours at your ship or your hideout is the definition of that condition.**

**Your ceiling is `10 + ranks + ability modifier`.** Anything at or under it you can build;
anything over it you cannot yet.

    a level-7 character with Repair 10 and Intelligence +2
    ceiling = 10 + 10 + 2 = 22
    builds anything DC 22 or lower, every time, no dice

**⚠ Read that precisely: not *"they fail"* — they cannot attempt it.** There is no failed
craft and no wasted material at a base. **The DC is a gate, not a test.**

### The source's own DCs become a career

**The games' own recipe table carries a DC from 1 to 32 for each recipe, ported unchanged**, and take-10
maps it onto this game's level curve without a number being touched:

| DC | What it buys | Ranks | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | common upgrades | 2 | 1 |
| 20 | good gear | 10 | 7 |
| 28 | rare | 18 | 15 |
| 32 | the best in the game | 22 | 19 |

**That progression is the source's, not ours.**

## When you do roll

**No eight hours, no base, or someone is shooting at you** — you cannot take 10, so you
roll `d20 + ranks + ability` against the same DC.

**On a failure the components are spent.**

> Which is the only place variance enters, and it is the place where variance is the point.

## Time — and what crafting competes with

**Owner ruling, and it is an addition: KOTOR has no time cost; this does.**

**One downtime period produces one crafting session, and no more than one per day.** A
session builds as many items as you have components for.

> **The rest is the cost, not the item.**

Ten stimulants and one lightsaber crystal cost the same rest, **because the constraint is
*you had an evening*, not *you performed ten operations*.**

**And it is the same downtime period that rest and meditation already draw from.** One
period, one choice among **rest, meditation, crafting, or droid construction** — never more
than one.

## Where — and the furniture that isn't

**A base: your ship, a hideout, anywhere the party holds and returns to.**

**Not a fixed workbench object.**

> KOTOR placed workbenches on maps because a video game needs a place to put the menu. **A
> table does not.**

**A GM who never places a workbench has not cut crafting.** That also removes an
encounter-design dependency three other systems had inherited.

## Who

**Anyone. Skill is the only gate, exactly as in the source.**

**And the `Machinist` keeps its identity without needing a lock:** it is **the only class
that can build a droid for someone else** — which is not on any recipe list, and which
Chapter Fourteen covers.

**⚠ Building a droid at all is not Machinist-only.** **`Droid Master` and `Engineer` can
both build one** — the two other classes whose whole premise is droids. What stays exclusively the Machinist's is building
*for another character*, and the `Repair` and `Scavenging` reduction on the parts bill.

## Breaking down

**Owner ruling: you get fewer components out than the item cost to make.**

> The galaxy's resources are infinite in the sense that you can go and find more. **They
> are not infinite in the sense that you can loop.**

**Self-regulating** — each breakdown-and-rebuild cycle loses material, so there is no craft
loop and **no need for a separate scarcity rule.**

## Six skills, not two

**KOTOR gated crafting on `Repair` and `Treat Injury` because it had two pieces of
furniture. This game does not have furniture.**

> **The gate is what the thing IS, not what bench you stood at.**

**The workbench list — 209 recipes — is `Repair`:** weapon and armour upgrades, shields,
melee and ranged components.

**The chemical list was never one skill.** Categorised from the blueprints:

| Recipes | What | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | trap kits and mines | **Demolitions** |
| 16 | implants | **split by effect — below** |
| 6 | adrenals and combat stimulants | **Science** |
| 6 | grenades — frag, ion, sonic, stun | **Demolitions** |
| 4 | medical equipment | **Medicine** |
| 3 | computer spikes | **Slicing** |
| 3 | air filters and breath masks | **Medicine** |

> **Of 73 "chemical" recipes, about seven are medicine. Thirty-one are explosives.**

**Medicine repairs a body. Science alters one.** Medpacs, antidotes, air filters and
medical equipment are Medicine; adrenals, stimulants and combat drugs are Science.

### Implants split by effect, and the blueprints decide it

**Not a judgement call** — every implant blueprint carries its property type and the split
derives from it:

    Ability bonuses — Strength, Constitution, Dexterity     Medicine
    Regeneration, immunities, damage resistance             Medicine
    Skill bonuses, granted feats, interface implants        Science

> **Grafting into a body is medicine. Augmenting beyond one is science.**

### And two obstacle skills finally build something

**`Demolitions` and `Slicing` were pure obstacle skills. Now they build.** Thirty-one of
the chemical recipes are theirs, which is a larger share than medicine's.

## ⚠ Five entries that are not recipes

`hkpart01` through `hkpart05` sit on the chemical list in the source:

    hkpart01   HK Droid Processor
    hkpart02   HK Chassis
    hkpart03   HK Control Cluster
    hkpart04   HK Vocabulator
    hkpart05   HK Protocol Pacifist Package

> **A processor, a chassis, a control cluster and a vocabulator is not an upgrade list. It
> is a whole droid, in parts.**

**KOTOR put them on the chemical bench because it needed somewhere to put *rebuild
HK-47*.** **These five are not recipes at all — they are droid construction, and they belong
with it in Chapter Fourteen.** Removed from the recipe count:
**73 becomes 68.**

## Sources for this chapter

**Part One — starting equipment.** The Player's Handbook is the authority; this chapter
summarises it so the whole roster can be seen at once. **Where the two differ, the Player's
Handbook governs.**

**Part Two — crafting.** The recipe lists, their DCs and the upgrade tables came straight
from KOTOR 2's own crafting data, **ported unchanged.** What this game added is the time
cost, the always-succeeds-at-a-base rule, the lossy breakdown, and **the reassignment of
recipes across six skills instead of two.**
