# Chapter Fifteen — Starships and Space Combat

**Status: DRAFT, for review.** The Armory's last chapter, and its largest subject — roughly
1,950 lines of source across three documents.

---

Every other chapter in this book equips a character. **This one equips the party.**

A ship is the only thing in the Armory that four people own together, that three other
systems quietly depend on, and that **cannot be bought with the first credits a party
earns** — because the rules say it has to be given.

---

# Part One — The Ships

## The player's ship

**The `Ebon Hawk` is a `Dynamic-class freighter`** — 25.5 metres, built by a subsidiary of
Core Galaxy Systems, and produced under a constraint that turns out to matter:

> *"Due to production inefficiency **only one vessel was made at a time.** This made them a
> rather rare sight."*

**The class is rare and the ship is unique, and those are two different facts** — the same
distinction the project draws between `HK-47` and the `HK-50` series.

> **A GM may give a party a `Dynamic-class freighter`. There is one `Ebon Hawk`.**

And the fiction licenses a party to make theirs their own: *"the ship was so heavily
modified that it was difficult to ascertain the ship's builder or origins."* **The famous
one is famous for being modified.**

## The first ship is not bought

> **A GM gives the party their first ship. They do not buy it.**

**Revan stole the `Ebon Hawk` from Davik Kang while Taris burned.** That is the shape:
**won, stolen, inherited, salvaged, or owed as a debt.**

**The price list below is for a ship merchant** — a second hull, a replacement, an upgrade.
**It is not a shopping list for act one.**

## Prices, and why they are not canon's

**Canon puts a used light freighter near 25,000 credits. The dearest single item in this
book is 32,000.**

> **At canon prices a starship costs less than one late-game rifle.**

So ships are priced against **this game's economy** instead:

    a tier-4 round of gear for four loadouts     ~100,000
    plausible lifetime wealth across 30 levels    300,000–500,000

**A ship costs more than a round of gear and less than a campaign.**

a party who sells one is giving up more than a hull.

| Vessel | Notional value |

| **Foray-class blockade runner** | 1.2 million |

| **Interdictor-class cruiser** | 6 million |
| **Kandosii-type dreadnaught** | 12 million |
| **Inexpugnable-class command ship** | 18 million |

> **These are for valuing a prize, a ransom or a wreck. No merchant sells one and no party
> buys one.**

**The Star Forge and the named flagships have no price. They are not property.**

## Contraband is a property of *you*, not of the ship

> ***"Not sold"* is not a property of the ship. It is a property of who you are.**

Every campaign package is written with a faction in mind, and both shipped packages are
Republic — **so the flags above are the Republic-campaign flags.**

    a Republic campaign      the Sith interceptor is contraband;
                             the Republic shuttle is military issue
    a Sith campaign          the reverse — the interceptor is requisitioned
                             and the Republic hull is the stolen one
    an Exchange campaign     both are merchandise, and the black-market
                             premium is the business model
    a Mandalorian campaign   the Shaadlar troopship is yours

**The numbers do not move.** Only whether a merchant will deal, and whether the purchase
asks questions.

## The ship as a place

> **A ship is a base that moves. That is the whole rule.**

**Chapter Twelve already defined a base** — *"your ship, a hideout, anywhere the party holds
and returns to"* — and defined it loosely on purpose. **This does not replace that. It says
what a ship adds.**

**What any base already gives you, unchanged:** crafting that always succeeds at eight
uninterrupted hours; free companion swaps at any rest or safe location; and somewhere held
droids sit.

**What a ship adds that a hideout does not:**

    it travels     the base goes WITH you. A hideout is somewhere you return
                   to; a ship is somewhere you leave from.
    it is private  nobody walks in. A departing companion leaves at the next
                   port, not mid-jump.
    it can be lost AND THIS IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS

> **A hideout that is raided is a setback. A ship that is lost strands the party, and every
> system above stops at once.**

**Crafting halts. Swapping halts. Held droids are wherever the ship is.** That is a real
stake **and it costs no new mechanic** — it is three existing rules losing their
precondition together.

### Losing the ship is authored, and the generator may never do it

**The reason is a softlock, not a preference.** Losing a ship is not a room or an encounter
— **it is a campaign state change**, and a generator could create the loss without creating
the way back.

**The recovery guarantee:** *if a campaign package takes the ship, it must state the way
back before it takes it.*

    RECOVERY     the same hull, returned — KOTOR's own answer
    REPLACEMENT  a different hull, given or earned
    SUBSTITUTE   a temporary base that carries the three systems above
                 until a hull exists again

**KOTOR did this twice and never left the player stranded.** The `Ebon Hawk` is taken at
Peragus and crashed on Malachor, **and both are authored with the return already written.**

> **A GM may take the ship for a scene, a session, or an arc. They may not take it without
> knowing how it comes back.**

**What a generator *may* do:** damage it — a repair is a cost, not a state change; strand it
somewhere, since the base still functions where it sits; put something aboard it. **It may
make the ship a problem. It may not make it absent.**

### Capacity, and a ship you do not own

**A ship carries the party, their companions, and every recruited companion not currently
fielded** — plus held droids, which are unlimited. **There is no weight limit**; carrying
capacity was killed and encumbrance deferred, **and a ship hold is not where either comes
back.** The `Dynamic-class` is 25.5 metres and KOTOR fit nine companions in one. **The
fiction does not count bunks and neither does this game.**

**Passage on someone else's ship is not a base.** You may rest; **you may not craft, swap,
or reach your droids.** That is the mechanical difference between having a ship and buying a
ticket — and it is why the first one is a gift rather than a purchase.

# Part Two — Space Combat

## What the source actually had

**Almost nothing, and it is worth being exact about how little:**

    SHIP STATS        nothing — 637 2DA files, no hull, no shields,
                      no speed column anywhere
    SPACE COMBAT      a rail shooter. keymap.2da gives its entire verb
                      set: MGshoot, and MGAction Up/Down/Left/Right.
                      Aim, and fire.
    ONE STATION       the gunner. The pilot is a cutscene.
    VEHICLE UPGRADES  one file — and it is a swoop bike

> **There is no turret system to extend. There is a control scheme with two verbs.**

**So this system is strictly more than the game ever had**, and the owner's brief is the
design: *"flying, with enemies, on a tabletop battle map. Instead of rails, **the pilot
moves**, and the other characters can be in whatever turrets there are. Whoever can't join
has to wait."*

## Two foundations the project already owned

**The swoop-bike upgrade table** supplies the grammar — three groups, four tiers each — and
it is the only vehicle-upgrade structure KOTOR shipped.

**And mounted combat supplies the rest.** A multi-station vehicle was already ruled and
playtested: *"only the first rider controls it. Everyone else is a passenger: they act on
their own initiative, with their own Action and Gear, and **no Move** — the mount's movement
is not theirs to spend."* Plus altitude bands and *"a flier may not reverse direction in a
round; it may turn up to 90 degrees, once."*

> **A ship is a mount with more seats.**

## The ship is one object. The crew are separate turns.

    THE PILOT     own initiative · MOVE spends the SHIP'S speed · ACTION is still theirs
    EACH GUNNER   own initiative · ACTION only · no Move
    THE CO-PILOT  own initiative · ACTION only

**The pilot keeps an action, and that is deliberate.** In mounted combat the *mount*
contributes the movement and the mount is not a player. **Here the pilot is a player.**

> **If flying consumed the whole turn, the pilot seat would be the worst seat on the ship.**

So it mirrors normal combat exactly: **move and action, every round. The move happens to
weigh 25 tonnes.**

## The map

    ONE SQUARE = 100 METRES

**Personal weapon ranges are 16–28 metres.** A blaster cannot reach one square, **which is
the point — hand weapons do nothing in space.**

    CLOSE    0–1 squares · boarding and ramming possible
    MEDIUM   2–6 · the default engagement band
    LONG     7+ · −2 to hit, both ways

## Movement, and the one roll that is not an attack

**A ship commits.** It may not reverse direction in a round, and may turn up to 90 degrees
**once**, at any point in its move. **That is the whole of manoeuvre, and it is deliberately
thin** — a facing-and-turn-radius system is a second game.

**The `Pilot` skill buys the exception:**

    DC 15    a second 90° turn this round
    DC 20    reverse direction — the thing a ship cannot otherwise do
    failure  the move ends where the check was made

> **This is why the pilot seat is a seat. Every other station rolls to hit; the pilot rolls
> to go somewhere the ship should not be able to go.**

## Damage — the action budget *is* the health bar

**A ship has Vitality like everything else.** One pool, **no separate shield track.**

    at half Vitality   one turret goes offline. The player in it waits,
                       like anyone without a seat.
    at zero            the ship is DISABLED, not destroyed

**A ship fight cannot end a campaign by accident** — losing the ship is authored only, and
that was the rule before this system existed.

**`Repair` at DC 20 brings an offline turret back for one round.** It costs the repairer's
action, and they must be aboard and unseated.

> **Which is the answer to *"whoever can't join has to wait."* They wait — until something
> breaks.**

## The seats, and what each one rolls

**The pilot** — `Pilot`, `Dex`-keyed, and **the only seat that rolls a skill.** Their action
is one of:

    EVASIVE        Pilot vs the attacker's Defence · one incoming attack misses
    CLOSE THE GAP  Pilot DC 15 · move a second time
    LINE UP        Pilot DC 15 · every gunner aboard gets +2 to hit until
                   your next turn

> **`LINE UP` is the one that makes the seat matter.** A pilot who spends their action on it
> is worth more than a gunner who fires.

**The gunner** — an **attack roll** against the target ship's Defence, using **the
character's own ranged attack bonus**, dealing **the turret's** damage rather than their
weapon's.

> **A turret is a weapon. So `BAB` applies, and a Soldier or Marksman is a better gunner
> than a Jedi Consular.**

**That is also the seating rule: the party puts its shooters in the turrets and its thinkers
in the cockpit.** Weapon-fighting feats do not apply — **a turret is one mounted gun, and
`Two-Weapon Fighting` has nothing to pair.**

**The co-pilot** — one of:

    ASSIST              +4 to the pilot's next Pilot check this round
    FORWARD GUN         an attack roll at the ship's fixed forward weapon
    TAKE THE CONTROLS   if the pilot is disabled or absent, you are the pilot

**`TAKE THE CONTROLS` is why the seat is not just a bonus.** It is the ship's redundancy,
and **it matters exactly once per fight, when it matters most.**

**The sensor seat** — `Awareness`, and **rare by design.** It finds a ship running dark, a
mine field, a contact beyond the map, **or a boarding attempt.** A sensor seat only matters
if there *is* something hidden, and most fights have nothing — **so a scout vessel has one
and a freighter does not.** The `Ebon Hawk` has none.

**The unseated** — `Repair`, and nothing else. **Deliberately thin.**

### The whole system in four lines

    PILOT      Pilot — a skill check
    CO-PILOT   +4 to someone else, or an attack roll
    GUNNER     an attack roll
    UNSEATED   Repair — a skill check

**Two skills and one attack. Nothing else is rolled in a ship fight.**

## The risk — how a character gets hurt in space

**The real gap was that a ship fight had no consequence for a *character*.** A ship could be
disabled and nobody aboard could be hurt.

> **A hit that beats the ship's Defence by 10 hits a station.** The station goes offline
> **and the character in it takes the turret's own damage die, Reflex save for half.**

**So a gunner can die in a ship fight**, and it is the same `−10` shape the rest of the game
already uses for critical confirmation.

**It also makes the seating decision real: the turret is the dangerous seat and the cockpit
is not.**

**And `Repair` now carries its own risk.** A character repairing a station **is at that
station.** If it is hit again, they are the one in it.

> That is what makes the unseated role a choice rather than a chore — **they are
> volunteering to stand in the place that just exploded.**

## Ship statistics

**Every number is derived from the 205 K1 creatures the project already parsed. A ship is a
CR-rated thing like everything else**, which means the encounter rules already price it.

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| **Vitality** | **10 × CR** |
| **Defence** | **10 + CR** |
| **Speed** | **4 squares** — 400 metres a round |

**`10 × CR` sits inside the game's own spread at every band** (21% mean absolute error across
14 bands). The real curve is not smooth — CR 6 sits below CR 5, CR 12 below CR 11 — **and a
design curve should not reproduce that noise.**

**Defence at `10 + CR` makes the station hit computable for the first time:** against an
even-CR opponent, beating Defence by 10 is **roughly one roll in ten.**

> **A gunner's seat explodes about once every ten incoming shots. Frequent enough to fear,
> rare enough not to dominate.**

**Speed 4 crosses the MEDIUM band in one move and cannot cross LONG** — which is what makes
`CLOSE THE GAP` worth an action.

### Turret damage — twice a personal weapon

    TIER 1   2d8        TIER 3   4d8
    TIER 2   3d8        TIER 4   5d8

A Blaster Rifle is `1d12`; a tier-1 turret is `2d8` — **average 9 against 6.5.** And **this
is also the damage a character takes when their station is hit**: `2d8` with a Reflex save
for half is survivable at level 1 and not ignorable.

### The Ebon Hawk, fully statted

    CR         the party's level
    VITALITY   10 × CR
    DEFENCE    10 + CR
    SPEED      4 squares
    SEATS      pilot · co-pilot · 1 turret
    TURRET     tier 1 · 2d8

> **The ship scales with the party.** A level-3 party flies a 30-Vitality Hawk; a level-15
> party flies a 150-Vitality one. **Same ship, same seats.**

**That is how KOTOR itself works — the `Ebon Hawk` is never upgraded and never outclassed.**

### And the balance becomes measurable

    a level-5 party:    ship has 50 Vitality, Defence 15
    its output:         1 turret 2d8 + 1 forward gun 2d8 = 18/round
    an even-CR enemy:   50 Vitality

**So a fight lasts three rounds** — **short, and short *because* the `Ebon Hawk` has two
guns.** The proof-of-concept ship is small, and **the fix is more seats on bigger ships, not
different numbers.**

# Part Three — What else is built

**Four further components exist in full and are summarised here rather than reproduced;
see Flag 3.**

**Upgrades** follow the swoop table's grammar — three groups, four tiers, `Accel` cut — with
prices anchored to the ship rather than to the party.

**Seats per hull class** turn the seat count into encounter design: **a fighter is a
one-player ship**, and a bigger hull is how the rest of the party gets something to do.

**Two fights you cannot win**, and both are deliberate: **a capital ship is terrain, not an
opponent**, and **a shuttle escape is a shared countdown.**

**Enemy ships by faction and role** — five combat roles, and each navy fights differently
*from its own lore* rather than from its statistics. **Boarding** has three ways aboard,
does not pause the ship fight, and **a captured party is a scene rather than a loss.**

**Ship parts** run six groups across four tiers — 24 parts — with **shields ruled as damage
reduction rather than a second pool**, consistent with the single-Vitality architecture.
**Specialist parts** give each builder one axis and no duplication: Duros hyperdrives,
Corellian modularity, Mandalorian boarding, Czerka selling to everyone.

## Open items, carried from review

**⚠⚠ Flag 1 — `STARSHIPS-01 §3` says space combat and ship stats are not built. Both are.**
Its *"What is NOT built"* list reads **`SPACE COMBAT — NOTHING`** and **`SHIP STATS —
NOTHING`**, with no ✓, while the same list marks travel time and the ship-as-base as closed.
**`SPACE-COMBAT-01` built both**: eight components at `PT-791`–`PT-808`, and ship stats
specifically at `PT-796` — Vitality `10 × CR`, Defence `10 + CR`, Speed 4.

**A reader consulting `STARSHIPS-01` alone would conclude this chapter's entire second half
does not exist.** **Reported, not fixed.**

**⚠ Flag 2 — `SPACE-COMBAT-01`'s own title contradicts its own status line.** The document
is titled ***"the plan, not the rules"*** and its status line immediately beneath reads
**"ALL EIGHT COMPONENTS BUILT."** The title is a fossil from when it was a plan. **Harmless
to anyone who reads two lines; misleading to anyone who reads one.**

**⚠⚠ Flag 3 — this chapter summarises four components rather than reproducing them, and
that is a judgement call worth your review.** Components 5 through 8 — upgrades, seats per
hull class, the unwinnable fights, enemy ships and boarding — plus the 24-part ship-parts
table and the specialist-parts material, run to several hundred lines of ruled content.

**Part Three names them and gives each its governing idea; it does not carry their tables.**
**My reasoning:** the Armory is an equipment book, and this chapter is already the longest
in it. **The enemy-ship roster and the ship-parts catalogue are catalogue material** of the
same kind Chapter Three carries for ranged weapons — **and they would be better as their own
chapter than as an appendix to this one.**

**Recommend a Chapter Sixteen** covering ship parts, specialist parts and the enemy roster.
**Say if you would rather it folded in here instead and I will expand Part Three.**

**⚠ Flag 4 — three numbers in the combat system are flagged as unverified in their own
source.** `SPACE-COMBAT-01` marks them itself: **whether `Repair` at DC 20 is the right
number**; **`LINE UP`'s `+2`, which is authored with no antecedent** (*"half of `Aid
Another`, because it reaches every gunner rather than one target"*); and the **`EVASIVE`
check, which `PT-879` converted from an opposed roll** — the only one in the system — to a
flat DC. **All three are the kind of number that only play settles.**

**⚠ Flag 5 — `STARSHIPS-01 §4` is headed *"Recommendation, not yet ruled"* and this chapter
does not draw on it.** Recorded so a later reader knows the omission is deliberate rather
than an oversight.

**Not a flag, but the pattern is now worth naming.** This is the **fifth** rules document in
four chapters carrying a superseded or self-contradicting passage alongside its replacement
— after `LOOT-01`'s duplicated paragraph, `DROID-CONSTRUCTION-01`'s retired part names and
flat rebuild cost, and `DROIDS-UPGRADE-01`'s self-documented stale paragraph. **Each
instance is minor and every one was caught by reading the source rather than the summary.**
**A sweep for "documents that describe their own content as unbuilt" would likely find
more.**
