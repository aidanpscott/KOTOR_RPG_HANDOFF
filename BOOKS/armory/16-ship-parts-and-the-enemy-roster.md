# Chapter Sixteen — Ship Parts and the Enemy Roster

**Status: DRAFT, for review.** Written on the recommendation made at the end of Chapter
Fifteen and approved: this is catalogue material of the same kind Chapter Three carries for
ranged weapons, and it belongs in its own chapter rather than as an appendix.

---

**Chapter Fifteen built the ship and the fight. This chapter is what you bolt to one and
what you point it at.**

# Part One — Ship Parts

## The shield ruling, which had to come first

**Adding shields threatened to reopen two settled rulings** — the single Vitality pool, and
the co-pilot seat that was cut because there were no ship systems to manage. **So shields
were ruled before any part was written:**

> **A shield part gives damage reduction. It does *not* add a track.**

**Both earlier rulings survive intact.** There is still one pool, and there is still nothing
for a systems officer to *manage* — **`DR` is passive.**

## Six groups, four tiers, 24 parts

**Prices follow the upgrade band:** 15,000 · 40,000 · 90,000 · **180,000.** **Tier 4 is
never sold** — at 180,000 credits it costs more than most ships, and nothing that expensive
sits on a shelf.

### 1 · Engine — speed

| Part | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Rendili Thrust Coupling** | Speed **5** |
| **Incom Vector Drive** | Speed **6** |
| **Aratech Burn Array** | Speed **7** |
| **Core Galaxy Slipstream Core** | Speed **8** — *crosses the `LONG` band in one move* |

### 2 · Frame — Vitality and Defence

| Part | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Reinforced Spaceframe** | `+2` Defence |
| **Bonded Hull Plating** | `+4` |
| **Republic Fleet Bulkheads** | `+6` |
| **Dreadnaught-Grade Spar** | `+8` Defence |

**The Frame line is quietly the most valuable**, and Chapter Fifteen explains why: the
station hit lands when an attack beats Defence by 10, which is about **one roll in ten**
against an even-CR opponent. **`+2` Defence makes it one in twenty.**

> **It buys your gunner's life, not your ship's.**

### 3 · Weapon — turret damage

| Part | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Czerka Gunnery Package** | `2d8` → **`3d8`** |
| **Aratech Cycler Bank** | **`4d8`** |
| **Republic Battery Mount** | **`5d8`** |
| **Mandalorian Siege Cannon** | **`6d8`** |

**One weapon upgrade cuts a level-5 party's fight from three rounds to two.** That is a real
benefit from **one** part, which is what the owner's brief asked for: *"a party may invest
in only a handful, or even none at all. But upgrades should offer real benefits."*

### 4 · Shield — damage reduction

| Part | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Deflector Screen** | `DR 2` |
| **Layered Particle Screen** | `DR 4` |
| **Kuati Ray Shielding** | `DR 6` |
| **Interdictor-Pattern Envelope** | `DR 8` |

### 5 · Turret — the action budget, and the strongest group in the game

| Part | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Dorsal Turret Ring** | **`+1` turret** |
| **Ventral Turret Ring** | `+1` more |
| **Quad Mount Conversion** | `+1` more |
| **Full Gunnery Deck** | `+1` more |

**Chapter Fifteen's central mechanic is that the turret count *is* the action budget.** So
**a part that adds a turret adds a player to the fight** — and the `Ebon Hawk` fights with
two attacks a round.

**⚠ These are priced on their own ladder — double the standard band:** **30,000 · 80,000 ·
180,000 · 360,000.**

### 6 · Sensor — range and effectiveness

| Part | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Scan Suite** | **Adds the sensor seat** to a ship without one |
| **Long-Range Array** | `+2` to `Awareness` aboard |
| **Sith Augury Package** | `+4` · *detects an Interdictor at `LONG`* |
| **Deep-Field Sensorium** | `+6` · **no enemy may open with a surprise round** |

**The sensor seat is rare by design and the `Ebon Hawk` has none.** **The tier-1 part is how
a party gets one** — which makes `Scan Suite` the only part in the book that adds a *seat*
rather than a number.

## And one group changes what the ship is

A freighter carries 1–2 turrets; a capital carries 4 or more. **Four turret parts take a
one-turret `Ebon Hawk` to five** — **a capital's seat count on a freighter's hull.**

**At 650,000 credits total, which is four times the price of the ship.**

> **So it is possible, and it is an entire campaign's money. That is the correct answer to a
> part that strong.**

# Part Two — Specialist Parts

**Six parts, and the test they had to pass is the interesting thing about them.** The
owner's constraint: *"provided they actually provide a natural benefit that others don't
offer."*

**So the 24 standard parts were swept first, and five axes came back untouched:**

    the pilot's own roll    nothing gave +Pilot
    the unseated            nothing helped Repair at DC 20
    boarding                there was no part at all
    stacking                one part per group, always
    the co-pilot            nothing touched ASSIST

**Each part below takes exactly one of them. Nothing here is a reskin**, and **a seventh
part would have to duplicate something** — which is why there are six.

## Duros — the first species to build a hyperdrive

| Part | Effect | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Duros Navigational Cortex** | **`+2` to every `Pilot` check aboard** | 60,000 |

**The only part that touches the pilot's own roll.** It improves the manoeuvre DCs **and**
`EVASIVE`, `CLOSE THE GAP` and `LINE UP` — **which means it indirectly helps every gunner.**

**The Duros built the first hyperdrive**, and `Pilot` is the skill that consolidated
`Astrogate`. **The part sits exactly there.**

## Corellian — modular by doctrine

| Part | Effect | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **CEC Modular Hardpoint** | **Mount a second part from one group** | 120,000 |
| **Corellian Damage-Control Runs** | `Repair` DC 20 → **DC 15** | 45,000 |

**⚠ The hardpoint is the only part in the game that breaks a structural rule.** Every other
part obeys *one per group*; **this one lets a ship carry two `Weapon` parts, or two
`Turret` parts.**

> Corellian Engineering's whole reputation is modularity — the `YT` line exists because
> every owner rebuilds it. **The part is the doctrine.**

**And the damage-control runs are the only part that helps the unseated.** Chapter Fifteen
made `Repair` the bravest job on the ship — *volunteering to stand in the place that just
exploded* — **and gave it no support.** This is the support.

## Mandalorian — *"we are already aboard"*

| Part | Effect | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Boarding Clamps** | **Board at half Vitality *or* dead engines — not both required — and you may board at `CLOSE` regardless** | 75,000 |
| **Ordo Pressure Bulkheads** | Enemy boarders arrive **one round late** | 45,000 |

**Boarding was gated and given no parts at all. These are both sides of it** — one buys the
attack, one buys the defence.

**A party that buys clamps is adopting someone else's way of fighting**, since boarding is
the Mandalorian doctrine.

### ⚠ And how a boarding action actually resolves

**This is the ruling that closed the whole item, and it is deliberately small:**

> **Boarding resolves as ordinary ground combat, not a new subsystem.**

The moment any gate triggers — close range, half Vitality, dead engines, or `Boarding
Clamps` overriding the range requirement — **the encounter hands off directly to standard
ground combat, narratively framed as taking place aboard the ship** rather than requiring
any new rules of its own.

**Combatants are whoever is actually aboard** — pilot, co-pilot, turret crew, any passengers
— **mapped onto real characters already built for everything else.**

**⚠ And the source is honest that this is a floor rather than a ceiling.** A real
ship-interior map — distinct areas a boarding fight could move through, the way KOTOR 2's
own scripted boarding sequence has real geography — **is wanted eventually and explicitly
not built now.** The narrative handoff is *"the honest floor, not a ceiling."*

## Czerka — it sells to everyone

| Part | Effect | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Czerka Crew Loop** | The co-pilot's `ASSIST` gives **`+6`, not `+4`** | 40,000 |

**The only part that touches the co-pilot.** And **it is Czerka because Czerka is a vendor
rather than a faction** — they sell the boring useful thing to both sides.

# Part Three — The Enemy Roster

## The two fighters that define the era

    SITH INTERCEPTOR   the thing you fight. The era's TIE fighter.
    AUREK-CLASS        the Republic's answer to it

**The `Aurek-class tactical strikefighter` covers all three of this setting's eras** — it
served in the Mandalorian Wars, the Jedi Civil War *"c. 3956 BBY"*, and the First Jedi
Purge — **first appeared in KOTOR 1, and flew at Rakata Prime.**

## One era per faction

| Faction | Fighter | Freighter | Capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Republic** | **Aurek-class** · S-250 Chela | Heraklon transport | **Hammerhead-class** |
| **Sith Empire** | **Sith interceptor** | — | **Interdictor-class** · Derriphan-class |
| **Mandalorians** | *none on the roster* | **Shaadlar troopship** | **Kandosii dreadnaught** · Kyramud battleship |
| **Exchange** | *bought, not built* | **Lethisk** | **none — it has no navy** |
| **Hutt Cartel** | *bought* | Lethisk · Heraklon | **none** |

**That table makes a setting point by itself.** The Exchange and the Hutt Cartel have **no
capitals and no fighter of their own. They fly what they bought.**

> Czerka is a vendor because it sells to everyone. **The criminal factions are the other
> side of that coin — they buy from everyone**, and a party that sees a `Lethisk` does not
> know whose it is.

## Pirates — a stat, not a faction

    PIRATES   any hull, one tier below its owner's
              no capitals · no organisation · no era window
              and never more than two hulls

> **A pirate is whatever they stole, badly maintained.** That is a stat rather than a
> faction: **take any ship and drop it a tier.**

**And it solves a real problem** — a GM who needs a space encounter at any date, any
location, with no faction implications, **has one.**

## CR by role

    FIGHTER    CR = party level − 2 · they come in numbers
    FREIGHTER  CR = party level
    CAPITAL    CR = party level + 5 · and it is terrain, not an opponent

**The crowd is already priced by a table written for ground combat, without modification:**
two creatures at `CR − 2`, four at `CR − 4`. **So four Sith interceptors at `CR − 4` is a
normal encounter.**

## Five combat roles

    GRUNT      CR − 4 · comes in fours · dies in one good hit
    HEAVY      CR + 0 · hits hard, turns badly
    SUPPORT    CR + 0 · does something that is not damage
    CARRIER    CR + 2 · releases grunts each round
    OBJECTIVE  CR + 5 · terrain, not an opponent

## And each navy fights differently — from its own lore

### The Sith — *"you cannot leave"*

**The `Interdictor-class cruiser` pulls ships out of hyperspace. That is its entire purpose,
and it is the faction's doctrine in one hull.**

    INTERDICTOR           SUPPORT · no escape is possible while it lives
    SITH INTERCEPTOR      GRUNT · in fours
    DERRIPHAN · CENTURION HEAVY

> **A Sith engagement is an ambush you cannot run from.** The shuttle-escape countdown does
> not work against them **until the Interdictor is dead.**

### The Mandalorians — *"we are already aboard"*

**The `Basilisk war droid` is ridden** *"down into the atmosphere of a world from space at
tremendous speeds, using gravitational force and the element of surprise."*

    BASILISK          GRUNT that CLOSES — it wants the CLOSE band
    DAVAAB-TYPE       GRUNT
    Q-CARRIER         CARRIER — it releases Basilisks
    SHAADLAR          troopship — boarding
    KANDOSII · KYRAMUD HEAVY

> **A Mandalorian fleet does not trade fire. It closes and boards, and every hull in the
> list serves that.**

### The Republic — *"we have an answer for that"*

    HAMMERHEAD      HEAVY · the Republic's line ship
    AUREK · CHELA   SCREEN — not a strike

**It is the only navy whose plan depends on yours.** Fighters answer fighters; the line ship
answers the line ship. And because *"commanders were sceptical of the ability of snubfighters
to decisively impact on a space battle,"* **its fighters screen and return — they do not
chase.**

### The Exchange and the Hutts — *"we do not fight"*

    LETHISK ARMED FREIGHTER   HEAVY, and there is only one
    no grunts · no carrier · no support

> **A criminal engagement is one armed ship and a threat. They would rather sell you
> something.**

## Why this matters more than the statistics

    vs SITH          kill the Interdictor — there is a trick
    vs MANDALORIANS  stay at range — there is a trick
    vs CRIMINALS     hurt them once — there is a trick
    vs REPUBLIC      there is no trick

> **Four factions, four different fights, and not one of them needed a new rule.**

**The Interdictor and the Basilisk do the same work from opposite ends:** one stops you
leaving, the other arrives whether you like it or not.

## Open items, carried from review

**⚠ Flag 1 — the parts list doubled and the earlier figure is still in the document.**
Component 5 specifies **three groups and "12 items to author"** — Engine, Frame, Weapon,
with `Accel` cut. **`PT-819` delivered six groups and 24 parts**, adding Shield, Turret and
Sensor. **That is growth rather than contradiction**, but *"12 items to author"* still reads
as current where it sits, and the true figure is **24 standard parts plus 6 specialist
parts — 30.**

**⚠ Flag 2 — the Turret group has its own price ladder and it is easy to miss.** Every other
group uses 15,000 / 40,000 / 90,000 / 180,000. **Turret parts are double throughout —
30,000 / 80,000 / 180,000 / 360,000** — which is why the full four-part conversion reaches
650,000. **Stated in the table above so a reader does not price a turret ring off the
standard band.**

**⚠ Flag 3 — two acknowledged gaps in the enemy roster.** The source names both:

- **No Mandalorian fighter is listed**, and **they fought a war.** The Basilisk fills the
  grunt role in the doctrine section, which may be the intended answer — but the roster
  table shows *none*, and the two are not reconciled in the document.
- **No Sith freighter is listed.** The note says *"they used captured ones"*, which is a
  reasonable in-fiction answer and leaves the table cell genuinely empty.

**⚠ Flag 4 — the Corellian hardpoint is the only part that breaks a structural rule, and
that is deliberate.** Every other part obeys *one per group*; the `CEC Modular Hardpoint`
permits a second from one group. **Recorded prominently because a rule with exactly one
exception is the kind of thing a later sweep "corrects" by mistake.**

**⚠ Flag 5 — boarding's resolution is explicitly a floor, and the source says so.** Boarding
hands off to ordinary ground combat with no new geography. **A real ship-interior map is
wanted and explicitly deferred** — *"the honest floor, not a ceiling."* **Recorded so the
simplicity reads as a decision rather than an omission.**

**Not a flag — the `Accel` group's fate is worth knowing.** The swoop table has Engine,
`Accel` and Frame; **`Accel` was cut** because *"acceleration matters in a race, where the
track is fixed and the clock decides. In a fight, speed is speed."* It **goes back on the
shelf for swoop racing, which is where it came from.**
