# Chapter One — What This Timeline Is, And Isn't

There is a reason this book opens with a chapter about itself rather than with the oldest
thing in it.

Every other book in this set describes something you cannot argue with. A vibrosword deals
its dice. A Defence value is a number or it is wrong. **History is not like that.** The
galaxy this campaign happens in has been written about for fifty years by hundreds of
hands — revised, contradicted, discontinued and restarted — and a great deal of what any
given reader confidently knows about it **is true of some other era, or of a version of
Star Wars this game does not use.**

So this chapter says what was let in, what was kept out, and — the part that matters most
— **what this book is not the authority on.**

## What this book is not the authority on

**Dates.**

That is not modesty, and it has a practical reason behind it.

**This book does not decide when things happened. It reports decisions made elsewhere**,
from sources you can go and check — the two games, the *Knights of the Old Republic
Campaign Guide*, the *Tales of the Jedi* comics, and the handful of reference volumes
listed later in this chapter. **Every year in these pages is a reading of those sources,
not a second copy of the record competing with the first.**

**So if a date here contradicts the sources, the sources win and this book has a mistake in
it** — and we would like to hear about it.

What this book supplies instead is the thing a date column cannot: **order, cause, and
consequence.** A table tells you the Mandalorian Wars ended in 3,960 BBY. It does not tell
you that Revan won them with a weapon that killed a world, and that the campaign you are
about to run is populated by people who were there.

## The four layers

Everything in Star Wars falls into one of four categories here, and the middle two are the
surprising ones:

| Layer | Material |
|---|---|
| **Authoritative** | KOTOR 1, KOTOR 2, *Tales of the Jedi* |
| **Not used, treated as never having happened** | *The Old Republic*, the novels, all Disney-era Star Wars |
| **Real, but irrelevant** | the original six films — **they have not happened yet** |
| **Left open for you** | KOTOR 3 |

**"Real, but irrelevant" is the category worth pausing on.** Nothing in this book disputes
that the films happened. **They happen four thousand years after its last page.** The
Empire is not yet a rumour; the Jedi Order is large, public and politically entangled in a
way it will never be again; and nobody has heard of the Skywalkers, because for another
four millennia there is nothing to hear.

**The films are not excluded. They are simply not yet news.**

**And KOTOR 3 is left open on purpose.** Where the sources stop, this game invents rather
than borrows — and anything invented is labelled as invented rather than presented as
something Star Wars already established. **That distinction — ours, and said to be ours,
as against inherited — runs through every book in this set.**

## The hazard is the opposite of the usual one

Most historical writing fights ignorance. **This book fights the opposite problem.**

The loudest, best-documented, most thoroughly catalogued Star Wars material — *The Old
Republic*'s decade of wiki coverage, the Disney-era films and series — **is precisely the
material this era cannot use.** Against it, the authoritative set is three sources: two
twenty-year-old games and a 1990s comic run.

**And the wrong material does not arrive announcing itself as wrong. It arrives as
something you already know.**

So the failure this book is written against is **not a blank page. It is a confident wrong
answer** — about the Sith Emperor, or Revan's later career, or what a Jedi temple looks
like. **Some corrections in these chapters will feel like they are contradicting Star
Wars.** They are contradicting *a different part of Star Wars*, and saying which part is
the whole job.

## Geography without history

This is where the distinction has to be exact, because the rule is narrower than *"The Old
Republic is out"* — and the difference carries real weight.

> **Places are admitted. Events and characters are not.**

A planet that first appeared in *The Old Republic* can be visited. What happened there
three centuries later cannot. The same applies to worlds from the films, the Thrawn novels
and elsewhere: **the location is usable, the history attached to it is not.**

**The reason is that the two age differently.** A world's position, its sector and its
hyperlane connections are stable across three centuries. **Its government, its population
and whose side it is on are not.**

### What this chapter is *not* doing

**Read the exclusion precisely, because it is narrower than it first sounds.** *The Old
Republic* is set aside **on the grounds that its events did not occur** — which is a
rejection of those events. **It is not a rejection of the material as a source of ideas,
and not of history it establishes as having already happened before 3,956 BBY.**

**So this chapter's framing changes nothing that has already been let in on that basis.**
The Flesh Raiders and the Rattataki are playable species in this game, and they stay
exactly as built. Ziost stands. The rules governing which species are available at the
campaign date stand.

**Each of those was admitted deliberately, one at a time, rather than by default** — and
passing that test is what being admitted means. **Where you want to know whether some
specific piece of that material is usable, the answer is a decision that was made and
recorded, not a matter of this chapter's tone.**

## How disagreements are settled

Setting and timeline facts run down a ranked ladder. **Where two sources conflict, the
higher rank governs — and the disagreement is written down rather than quietly resolved in
favour of the loser.**

| Rank | Source |
|---|---|
| 1 | **KOTOR 1 and KOTOR 2** |
| 2 | *Knights of the Old Republic Campaign Guide* |
| 3 | KOTOR comics and *Tales of the Jedi* |
| 4 | *The Essential Atlas* |
| 5 | *Dark Empire* |
| 6 | *The New Essential Chronology* |
| 7 | Wookieepedia — **a route to whichever ranked source underlies a claim, not a source itself** |
| 8 | *Jedi vs. Sith*; *The New Essential Guide to Alien Species* — **descriptive lore only, never rules, nothing after 3,956 BBY** |

**Rank 7 is not a source so much as a signpost.** A wiki article is useful for finding
*which* published work a claim comes from; the claim then stands or falls on that work's
own rank. **A fact that can only be traced to the wiki is a fact still waiting for a
source.**

### A worked example

**The Legends wiki dates the Sith attack on Telos IV to 3,959 BBY**, tracing it to *The
Essential Atlas* — rank 4. **The Campaign Guide says 3,958 BBY** — rank 2.

**3,958 governs, and both dates are on record.** Nobody deleted the losing one. It sits
where the next person to wonder can see that the question was asked and how it came out.

### And a separate ladder governs the rules

The games rank first for *what happened in the galaxy*. For *what a weapon does*, they do
not rank at all.

> **A game supplies what happened in the galaxy. It never supplies what a mechanic does.**

That is why this book and the Armory can disagree about a weapon without either being
wrong. **They are answering different questions from different sources.**

## What is refused — and why it is a test rather than a list

**There is no banned-worlds list.** There are tests, so that a world nobody anticipated
still gets a correct answer:

| A world is out if | Because |
|---|---|
| It was founded after 3,956 BBY | It does not exist yet |
| It was discovered after 3,956 BBY | Nobody has charted it yet |
| It first appeared in print after April 2014 | It belongs to a different continuity |
| It is a creature of the 3,640 BBY era | Wrong century, and a contaminated one |
| Its situation changes across 3,956 BBY | **See below — this one is not a ban** |

**That last class is not an exclusion.** Those worlds are reachable. **They are simply not
in the state most people picture them in** — because the version most readers know is the
one from a later century.

### The April 2014 line is a continuity, not a birthday

**Legends is one coherent body of material that was written to fit together.** What came
after the 2014 reboot is a different one, and it makes no claims this game wants.

**And that line has been tested and corrected once, which is the best evidence it is a real
rule rather than a preference.** An earlier version of it used **2010** as the cutoff — a
rough stand-in for *before The Old Republic's lore programme began.* **It failed on its
first real test.**

***Dawn of the Jedi*** ran from 2012 to 2014, is set at 25,793 BBY, and takes the Rakatan
Infinite Empire as its antagonist. **By date it is post-*Old Republic*. By subject it is
twenty-two thousand years clear of it.** The date was a proxy; the question it stood in for
was *does this material depend on the era we are excluding?* **When the two disagree, the
question governs and the date gives way.**

## Deep history plays by looser rules

One more exception, and Chapter Two runs entirely on it.

**Material describing events before roughly 4,000 BBY is admitted on looser terms**, for a
straightforward reason: **it cannot collide with anything.** A claim about 5,000 BBY
changes nothing during play. Nothing at the founding of the Sith Empire is going to
happen, be prevented, or come due in the middle of a campaign set in 3,956 BBY.

> **So deep history is background rather than chronology. It needs to be plausible and
> evocative rather than precisely dated.**

**Chapter Two is therefore a different kind of chapter from the ones after it** — written
for shape and causation rather than for a dated spine, and deliberately vaguer about years
than Chapters Four through Seven. **Claiming more precision than the sources carry would be
the error, not the service.**

**And the looseness does not spread.** It applies to *distance*, not to the window.
Everything from roughly 4,000 BBY forward — which is to say the rest of this book — runs on
the full ranked ladder exactly as before.

## What this book is, in one paragraph

**A reader's companion to a settled record, not the record itself.** It explains how the
galaxy got to 3,956 BBY, in what order and for what reasons, using the best-ranked source
available for each claim and telling you which one that was. Where the sources disagree it
shows the disagreement. Where they are silent it says so, rather than filling the gap with
the loudest thing nearby. **And where it gives a date, that date came from somewhere you
can check — which will still be right if this page is wrong.**
