# Chapter One — What This Timeline Is, And Isn't

**Status: DRAFT, for review.** First chapter of the Galactic Timeline, written after the
sourcing assessment at `TO-MAIN-44-AUTHOR.md` and the rulings that closed it.

---

There is a reason this book opens with a chapter about itself rather than with the oldest
thing in it.

Every other book in this set describes something the reader cannot argue with. A
vibrosword deals its dice. A Defence value is a number or it is wrong. **History is not
like that.** The galaxy this campaign happens in has been written about for fifty years by
hundreds of hands, revised, contradicted, discontinued and restarted, and a great deal of
what any given reader confidently knows about it **is true of some other era, or of a
continuity this project does not use.**

So this chapter says what was admitted, what was refused, and — the part that matters most
— **what this book is not the authority on.**

## What this book is not the authority on

**Dates.**

That is not modesty. It is the project's own rule, and it exists for a specific reason.
`CANON-01-v2 §4` holds one global timeline as engine state, *"queried, never recalled from
prose,"* and gives the reason plainly:

> **Dates live in engine state**, injected at resolution time. Dates living only in pack
> prose get silently "corrected" by the model against a chronology it half-remembers.

**This book is prose containing dates.** Read the rule literally and it describes this
book's central hazard. Every year in these chapters is a *reading* of the engine's state
and the ranked sources behind it, not a second copy of the record competing with the
first. Where this book and the engine disagree, **the engine is right and this book has a
bug** — and the project would like to hear about it.

What this book supplies instead is the thing engine state cannot: **order, cause, and
consequence.** A date column tells you the Mandalorian Wars ended in 3,960 BBY. It does
not tell you that Revan won them with a weapon that killed a world, and that the campaign
you are about to run is populated by people who were there.

## The four layers

`CANON-01-v2 §2` sorts everything into four categories, and the second and third are the
surprising ones:

| Layer | Material |
|---|---|
| **Authoritative** | KOTOR 1, KOTOR 2, *Tales of the Jedi* |
| **Not canon, treated as non-existent** | SWTOR, the novels, all Disney-era Star Wars |
| **Canon but irrelevant** | the original six Lucas films — **they have not happened yet** |
| **Open for user invention** | KOTOR 3 |

**"Canon but irrelevant" is the category worth pausing on.** Nothing in this book disputes
that the films happened. They happen *four thousand years after the last page of it.* The
Empire is not yet a rumour; the Jedi Order is large, public and politically entangled in a
way it will never be again; and nobody has heard of the Skywalkers, because for another
four millennia there is nothing to hear. **The films are not excluded. They are simply not
yet news.**

**And KOTOR 3 is left open on purpose.** Where the sources stop, this project invents
rather than imports — *"a package may draw ideas from SWTOR provided it declares them as
its own assertions rather than inherited canon."* That distinction — **ours, declared, as
against inherited** — runs through the whole project, and it is the same discipline the
Armory uses when it marks an authored weapon `⚠ AUTHORED` instead of passing it off as
extracted.

## The hazard is inverted from the usual case

Most historical writing fights ignorance. **This book fights the opposite problem**, and
`CANON-01-v2 §2.1` names it exactly:

> The model's priors are dominated by exactly the excluded material. SWTOR has a decade of
> wiki coverage; Disney canon is the most recent and reinforced layer. The authoritative
> set is three sources, two of them twenty-year-old games and one a 1990s comic run.

**The source record says this about the engine. It is just as true of the reader.** The
loudest, best-documented, most-reinforced Star Wars material is precisely the material
this era cannot use — and it does not arrive announcing itself as wrong. It arrives as
something you already know.

So the failure mode this book is written against is **not a blank page. It is a confident
wrong answer**, delivered fluently, about the Sith Emperor or Revan's later career or what
a Jedi temple looks like. The corrections in these chapters will sometimes feel like they
are contradicting Star Wars. They are contradicting **a different part of Star Wars**, and
saying which part is the whole job.

## Geography without history — and what this chapter is *not* doing

This is where the distinction has to be exact, because the rule is narrower than
"SWTOR is out" and the difference is load-bearing.

`CANON-01-v2 §2.2`:

> SWTOR planets are available as places; SWTOR events and characters are not. Pre-Disney
> planets from the films, Thrawn novels, and elsewhere are likewise available as geography.
>
> State this as an explicit pack rule, or the AI will import a planet together with its
> history and the failure will present as a retrieval bug rather than a policy gap.

`WORLDS-REGISTER-01` puts the same rule in four words — **"Geography admitted, state
excluded"** — with the reasoning that *"a world's location, sector, and hyperlane position
are stable across three centuries; its government, population, and factional alignment are
not."*

**⚠ So read `§10.0`'s exclusion precisely.** SWTOR is excluded *"on the basis that the
events of that game did not occur — **a rejection of its events, not of the material as a
source of ideas, and not of history it establishes as already having happened before the
campaign date.**"*

**This chapter's framing changes nothing that has already been admitted on that basis.**
Flesh Raiders and Rattataki are playable species in this project's own Species material,
ruled in and recorded — the Flesh Raiders through `DECISION-RECORD-04`'s `D-Q`, locked
against the Rakata record, and they stay exactly as built. The Ziost admission stands. The
engine's availability-window wiring stands. **Each of those went through the gate `§10.0`
requires** — *"nothing on that basis is admitted by default; each item requires the owner's
approval"* — and passing that gate is what admission means.

**The book's stance and the project's ledger are two different things, and only one of
them is a rules document.** This is the narrative frame; the ledger is the record. Where a
reader wants to know whether some piece of SWTOR material is usable, **the answer is in the
ledger, never in this chapter's tone.**

## How disagreements are settled

Setting and timeline facts run down a ranked ladder. Where two sources conflict, **the
higher rank governs, and the disagreement is written down rather than quietly resolved
downward.**

| Rank | Source |
|---|---|
| 1 | KOTOR 1 and KOTOR 2 |
| 2 | KOTOR Campaign Guide |
| 3 | KOTOR comics and *Tales of the Jedi* |
| 4 | *The Essential Atlas* |
| 5 | *Dark Empire* |
| 6 | *The New Essential Chronology* |
| 7 | *(reserved — `D-W32`'s local Wookieepedia extension, worlds register only)* |
| 8 | *Jedi vs. Sith*; *The New Essential Guide to Alien Species* — **descriptive lore only, never mechanics, nothing after 3,956 BBY** (`PT-1800`) |

**⚠ This table is a reading, not the record.** `D-W32` in `WORLDS-REGISTER-01` rules that
documents should **cite** the hierarchy at `METHOD-RECORD-01 §2` and `D-AB` rather than
restate it, precisely so that copies cannot drift — and the drift is not hypothetical.
Two instances are recorded in the flags below. **A reader settling a real conflict should
go to `METHOD-RECORD-01 §2`, not to this page.**

**A worked example of the ladder doing its job**, from the worlds register: the Legends
wiki dates Telos IV to 3,959 BBY, tracing to the *Essential Atlas* at rank 4. The Campaign
Guide says 3,958 BBY at rank 2. **3,958 governs, and both are recorded.** Nobody deleted
the losing date; it sits in the register where the next person to wonder can see that the
question was asked and how it came out.

**A separate and disjoint hierarchy governs rules**, and the games are not in it at all:

> **A game supplies what happened in the galaxy. It never supplies what a mechanic does.**

That sentence is why this book and the Armory can disagree about a weapon without either
being wrong — they are answering different questions from different ladders.

## What is refused, and why it is a test rather than a list

The project does not keep a banned-worlds list. It keeps **predicates**, so that a world
nobody anticipated still gets a correct answer. `WORLDS-REGISTER-01`'s `D-W3`:

| Exclusion class | Predicate |
|---|---|
| Not yet settled | founding date > 3956 BBY |
| Not yet charted | discovery date > 3956 BBY |
| Out of continuity window | first publication after April 2014 |
| Contaminated era | SWTOR-era, ~3640 BBY |
| Exists, wrong state | state change crosses 3956 BBY |

**The last class is not a ban**, and the register says so directly: *"Those worlds are
reachable — just not in the state the narrator's training data describes."*

**The April 2014 line is a continuity, not a birthday.** `METHOD-RECORD-01 §3`:

> **The bound is a continuity, not a year.** Legends is one coherent body of material that
> was written to fit together. Post-reboot canon is a different one, and it makes no claims
> this project wants.

And that bound has already been tested and corrected once, which is the best evidence it
is a real rule rather than a preference. An earlier **2010** bound was a proxy for *before
SWTOR's lore programme* — and it **produced a false positive on its first real test.**
*Dawn of the Jedi* ran 2012–2014, is set at 25,793 BBY, and takes the Rakatan Infinite
Empire as its antagonist: post-SWTOR by publication, twenty-two thousand years clear of it
by subject. The rule that survived:

> **The dependency test was always the rule and the date was always the proxy.** When the
> two disagree, the test governs.

## Deep history plays by different rules

One more exception, and Chapter Two runs entirely on it. Material describing events
**before roughly 4,000 BBY** is admitted on looser terms, because *"a claim about 5000 BBY
produces no temporal facts. Nothing at the founding of the Sith Empire changes state during
play. It cannot be pending, cannot fire, cannot leak, and cannot be averted."*

> **So deep history is world-bible content, not timeline content**, and the entire
> scheduling apparatus is irrelevant to it. **It needs to be plausible and evocative rather
> than dated and tracked.**

**Chapter Two is therefore a different kind of chapter from the ones after it** — written
for shape and causation rather than for a dated spine, and deliberately vaguer about years
than Chapters Four through Seven, because claiming more precision than the sources carry
would be the error, not the service.

**And the looseness does not spread.** *"Looser terms apply to distance, not to the
window."* Everything from roughly 4,000 BBY forward — which is to say the rest of this book
— is governed by the full ranked ladder exactly as before.

## What this book is, in one paragraph

**A reader's companion to a settled record, not the record.** It explains how the galaxy
got to 3,956 BBY, in what order and for what reasons, using the highest-ranked source
available for each claim and saying so. Where the sources disagree it shows the
disagreement. Where they are silent it says they are silent, rather than filling the gap
with the loudest thing nearby. And where it states a date, that date belongs to the engine,
which will still be right if this page is wrong.

## Open items, carried from review

**⚠ Flag 1 — `D-W32` drift, instance one.** `METHOD-RECORD-01` line 310 argues the New
Essential Chronology's usefulness *"despite ranking fifth,"* while every ladder in the
corpus — including the one about thirty lines above it in the same document — places it
**sixth**. A stale numeral left behind when `D-T` inserted *Dark Empire* at rank 5. The
argument is unaffected. Reported, not fixed; editing a rules document to correct a numeral
is not this book's call.

**✔ Flag 2 — the "rank 7" collision, raised and now closed by `PT-1800`.** Recorded here
because the resolution is load-bearing for every citation in this book.

`WORLDS-REGISTER-01`'s `D-W32` assigns **Wookieepedia** a *local* rank 7 — *"For Atlas
purposes only… a local extension, not a change to `D-AB`"* — and that rank is live in the
register, carrying the Bith and Glee Anselm warrants, with `D-W42` recording others
**upgraded off rank 7 to rank 2**. Placing the two Essential Guides at rank 7 as well
would have put **two very different things under one label**: a wiki route meant to be
escalated off as soon as an underlying source is named, and an admitted Legends reference
book with a stable citation and nothing pending. A warrant reading "rank 7" would have
been ambiguous, and the wiki sense carries an active *needs-escalating* connotation the
reference books should not inherit.

**`PT-1800` moves them to rank 8**, leaving rank 7 to `D-W32`'s local extension. The
labels now separate cleanly and `D-W42`'s escalation history keeps its meaning.

**⚠ Flag 3 — the Campaign Guide OCR's page markers are not printed folios, and citations
built on them will be wrong by three.** `data/books/KOTOR-CG-OCR.txt` marks pages
`=== PAGE n ===`, but pages 1–3 are the cover, title page and credits, which carry no
printed folio. **Printed folio = OCR marker − 3.** Verified against two existing corpus
citations, both exact: `D-W42` cites *"Timeline p.113"* for Aleema Keto and the Cron
Cluster, which sits at OCR `PAGE 116`; `OUTLINE-02` gives the Campaign Guide timeline as
**pp. 112–113**, which spans OCR `PAGE 115`–`116`. **⚠ I reported this file as
"page markers enabling folio citation" in `TO-MAIN-44-AUTHOR.md` without stating the
offset** — true but incomplete, and anyone acting on it would have cited three pages high.
Correcting it here. This book's citations use printed folio.

**⚠ Flag 4 — `OUTLINE-02` cites `CANON-01-v2 §3.4` for this chapter; §3.4 is the wrong
section.** It is *"`requires` is a predicate, not a list,"* about the canon ledger's
requires predicate — nothing to do with how the timeline was built. The sections this
chapter actually stands on are **§2, §2.1, §2.2, §4 and §10.0**. Same class of error as
the `D-CURRENCY-01` currency miscitation found in the Armory.

**✔ Fixed.** `OUTLINE-02`'s Book Five row now cites `§§2, 2.1, 2.2, 4, 10.0` and records the
superseded citation.

**Correction to my own sourcing report.** `TO-MAIN-44-AUTHOR.md` stated the Campaign Guide
timeline runs *"4,000 BBY through 3,976 BBY."* **It runs 4,000 BBY through 3,950 BBY** —
fifty years, not twenty-four — on pp. 112–113, and it covers the Mandalorian Wars, the
Jedi Civil War, the Sith Triumvirate and the start of the Reconstruction in dated entries.
**Chapters Four through Seven are better sourced than I reported**, and none of them needs
the reference books.
