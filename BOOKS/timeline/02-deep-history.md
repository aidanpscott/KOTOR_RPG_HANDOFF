# Chapter Two — Deep History

**This chapter is written to a different standard from the ones after it, and the
difference is deliberate.**

Everything from Chapter Three onward is a dated spine — years, order, and consequences a
campaign can collide with. **This chapter is not that.**

**Material from before roughly 4,000 BBY is held to looser terms for a straightforward
reason: none of it can reach your table.** Nothing at the founding of the Sith Empire is
going to come due during a campaign set in 3,956 BBY, be set off by something the party
does, or be prevented by them. **It is background rather than chronology, and it needs to
be plausible and evocative rather than precisely dated.**

**It also has no Major Figures section, unlike the chapters that follow.** A formal roster
would fight that looser standard, so Adas, Ajunta Pall and the rest stay woven into the
account.

**So the dates here are approximations and are meant to be read as approximations.** Where
the sources give a round number, this chapter gives the round number. Where they give
relations instead — *three thousand years before the founding of the Republic* — this
chapter keeps the relation rather than converting it into a false precision. Claiming more
than the record carries would be the error here, not the service.

What this chapter is for is the **shape** of it: why there is a Star Forge orbiting a sun
in the Unknown Regions, why Korriban is full of tombs, and why a Dark Lord in 3,956 BBY is
the inheritor of something that was already ancient when the Republic was founded.

## The Infinite Empire

Long before the Republic, one species held more of the galaxy than anyone has held since.

The **Rakata** built an empire that *"once stretched from the unknown regions to distant
parts of the galaxy, such as Corellia and Manaan,"* and they called it the **Infinite
Empire** — a name that reads less as boast than as description to anyone living under it.
At its height they built the **Star Forge**, *"a massive space station factory that enabled
them to create the machinery and ships needed to manage their massive, interstellar
empire,"* drawing its energy directly from the sun of the Lehon system.

**The crucial fact about the Rakata is the one that eventually destroys them:** *"The Force
powered their technology, and they relied on their connection to the Force to control
it."* Their ships, their factory, their hold on a thousand subject worlds — all of it ran
on the Force. They did not have a technology that the Force assisted. They had a
technology that was **nothing without it.**

It is worth sitting with how strange that is. An empire of that reach, built on a
foundation that could in principle simply stop.

**This is also where one of this game's playable species comes from.** The Flesh Raiders are
Rakata — *"mutated, selectively bred"* descendants produced by the Infinite Empire as
lower-caste soldiery, and they carry the marks of it still, down to a Force-blindness
inherited from their parent stock. Whatever the Rakata did to the galaxy, they did a
version of it to their own kind first.

## Korriban, and the king who beat them

Meanwhile, on a world the Republic had not yet heard of, something else was already old.

> The story of the Sith goes back one hundred thousand years, eons before even the rise of
> the Republic or the Jedi.

**The Sith were a species and a people before they were ever a religion** — a distinction
that matters more than almost anything else in this book, because in 3,956 BBY the word
means a Force tradition, and here it means a population with a homeworld. That homeworld
was **Korriban.**

Around **28,000 BBY**, *"the archetypal model of ambition rises to power among them."* He
unites Korriban into a single kingdom and claims the titles of *"both monarch and god of
his people."* History calls him **King Adas**, though legend gives him another name:
**Sith'ari — the perfect being.**

Then the Infinite Empire arrives.

*"Three thousand years before the founding of the Republic, Adas's hegemony is invaded by
the Rakatan Infinite Empire, an immensely powerful interstellar nation."* And the thing
that should have happened did not happen.

> Here, later Sith would say, they prove their right to rule the galaxy. **Outsmarting the
> technologically superior invaders, Adas conquers the conquerors, sacrificing his life in
> the process.**

**Adas wins and dies doing it** — which is precisely the sort of founding story a
civilisation built on strength would keep. With captured Rakatan technology the Sith
prosper, colonise nearby worlds, and banish a group of pureblooded Sith as far away as
**Tund.**

**But the source names a second inheritance, and it is the larger one:**

> But there is a greater gift the Infinite Empire bequeathed to the Sith — **the dark side
> of the Force.**

Everything else in this book runs downhill from that sentence. The Great Sith War, Exar
Kun, Revan and Malak, the Triumvirate — all of it descends from a technology the Rakata
ran their empire on, handed to the people who beat them.

## The fall

**The Campaign Guide, the highest-ranked source that speaks to this at all, declines to
say why it happened:**

> **For unknown reasons**, the Rakata eventually lost their ability to use the Force. As
> their grasp over their machinery and technology weakened, so did their control over
> their subjugated worlds. The native species managed to throw off their masters and
> regain control of their own destinies.

**⚠ *The New Essential Chronology* supplies a cause, and because it ranks below the
Campaign Guide this chapter presents it as an account rather than as the fact.** By that
telling, around **25,200 BBY** a Force-negating plague swept the Rakata — its effect
worsened by their own genetic uniformity — and crippled the connection their technology
depended on. What followed was galaxy-wide slave revolt and civil war, and an empire that
had stood for millennia came apart in a short span.

**The two accounts do not actually collide.** Rank 2 says the cause is unknown; it does not
say there was none. Rank 6 fills a gap the higher source left open, which is the ladder
working normally rather than a conflict to be resolved. **A reader should hold the collapse
as certain and the plague as the best available explanation**, and this chapter writes it
that way.

However it began, the ending is the same, and the Campaign Guide's account of it is
merciless:

> The Rakata and their planet became lost to history, **perhaps purposefully erased by the
> newly freed societies.** Their former subjects eliminated their former masters, or forced
> them to leave. Rakata civilization on their homeworld of Lehon deteriorated until all
> that remained were small, primitive tribes.

**An empire that spanned the galaxy ends as tribes on a beach, on a planet nobody can
find.** Not destroyed by a rival — destroyed by the withdrawal of the one thing it was
built on, and then deliberately forgotten by the people it had held down.

## What rose in the quiet

**⚠ This section rests on *The New Essential Chronology* alone — rank 6.** The Campaign
Guide contains no reference to Tython, and none to Corbos; the whole of its 21,000-line
text was searched for both. **If either matters to your campaign, the Chronology is the
volume to check.**

The centuries after the collapse are not a single documented thread, and the Chronology
does not pretend otherwise. Two developments run roughly alongside each other: the rise of
the **Galactic Republic**, and the establishment of the **Jedi Order on Tython.** They are
contemporaneous rather than causally chained, and anyone wanting a tidy sequence here is
asking the record for something it does not hold.

What matters for this book is only that both exist by the time the next disaster arrives.

## The Hundred-Year Darkness, and the exiles who became the Sith

**The Jedi Order's first great catastrophe was internal.** A faction turned to the dark
side, and the schism became a war — the **Hundred-Year Darkness.** By the Chronology's
account it ends at the **Battle of Corbos**, after which the defeated Dark Jedi are exiled
from Republic space altogether. *(Corbos is rank 6 only — see the note above.)*

**Exile has to lead somewhere, and where it led is the hinge of the entire setting.** The
Campaign Guide takes the story back at rank 2:

> Eventually, the Sith are again visited by aliens, this time by the Dark Jedi exiled in
> the Hundred-Year Darkness. These exiles, initially called **Jen'jidai** in the Sith
> tongue, easily conquer the reigning Sith overlord, earning their leader **Ajunta Pall**
> the new title **Jen'ari** or "Dark Lord" of the Sith.

**Note what actually happened there, because it is almost always told wrong.** The exiles
did not found the Sith. They *arrived* at a Sith civilisation that Adas had united an age
earlier — before the Republic existed — that had already beaten the Infinite Empire, and
that had held the dark side since. **They conquered it and took its name.**

What was born at Korriban was not the Sith people — those were there first — but **the Sith
as a Force tradition**: a fusion of exiled Jedi and an existing dark-side civilisation,
under a title, *Dark Lord*, that the conquered supplied for their conquerors.

And the fusion was literal. Some conquerors *"follow their leaders back to Republic space
for revenge against the Jedi,"* but *"many stay to interbreed with the Sith."* Every Sith
Lord in this book's later chapters stands at the end of that line.

## What deep history leaves lying around

**The reason any of this belongs in a campaign book rather than an appendix is that none of
it is safely finished.**

The Infinite Empire left *"a legacy of artifacts of their advanced civilization that are
just waiting to be found"* — and the Campaign Guide is explicit that this is a feature
rather than colour, offering *"a blank slate on which to design adventure hooks."* Rakatan
technology may surface as *"powerful weapons"* or as *"advanced medical technology or
methods of power generation unparalleled by existing technology."*

**And the Star Forge is still there.** *"The most influential piece of Rakatan technology
known to exist in the Knights of the Old Republic period"* — a factory that mass-produces
warships, orbiting a sun, waiting more than twenty thousand years for someone to find the
map back to it.

**Somebody does.** Revan finds a Rakatan star map on Kashyyyk in 3,961 BBY (Chapter Four)
and the war ends at Lehon four years later (Chapter Five) — **the point at which deep
history stops being background and starts being the plot.**
