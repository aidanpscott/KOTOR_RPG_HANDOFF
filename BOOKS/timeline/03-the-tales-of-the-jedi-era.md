# Chapter Three — The Tales of the Jedi Era

**Forty years before this campaign begins, the galaxy nearly ended, and everyone who
matters in 3,956 BBY is standing in the wreckage.**

That is the whole reason this chapter exists. The Great Sith War is not background: it is
the event that emptied the Jedi Order, broke the Republic's economy, taught the
Mandalorians that the Republic could be beaten, and left Korriban in Sith hands when the
shooting stopped. **Every faction in this book's later chapters is doing something that
only makes sense as a response to these four years.**

It is also the last time the galaxy faced a Sith war and *won cleanly enough to forget the
lesson.*

---

## The events

### 4,000 BBY — two aristocrats find something on Empress Teta

It does not begin with a Sith Lord. It begins with a pair of bored nobles.

**Satal and Aleema Keto** *"discover ancient secrets of the Sith, including artifacts and
tomes of knowledge, on their home planet of Empress Teta,"* and do the worst possible thing
with them: they **recruit.** Hundreds of fellow aristocrats join what the Ketos name the
**Krath** — *"a dark side cult based on Sith teachings."*

**For three years nothing visible happens.** That gap is the most instructive thing in the
early timeline. A dark-side movement inside a wealthy, well-connected ruling class did not
announce itself, and nobody stopped it, because there was nothing yet to stop.

### 3,997 BBY — the year everything breaks at once

Then the Krath move, and four separate catastrophes land inside a single year.

**The coup.** *"Steeped in knowledge of the dark side of the Force, the Krath rally military
forces throughout the Deep Core and stage a coup on their own homeworld of Empress Teta."*
The cult now owns a world and its fleet.

**Deneba.** *"The Jedi are ambushed by Krath forces during a conclave on the planet Deneba,
causing massive Jedi casualties."* The Order had gathered precisely to decide what to do
about the Krath, and the Krath attacked the gathering. **Ulic Qel-Droma leaves Deneba with
a plan** — to *"infiltrate the upper echelons of the Krath"* from the inside.

**Korriban.** Separately, and for his own reasons, a Jedi apprentice named **Exar Kun**
*"abandons his master to delve deeper into the secrets of the dark side, traveling the
galaxy in search of Sith lore. On Korriban, Kun is educated in the ways of the Sith and
soon is elevated to the rank of Dark Lord of the Sith."*

**The duel that didn't happen.** Ulic's infiltration fails in the way infiltrations fail:
*"After the Jedi attempt to extract him from the Krath-held world of Empress Teta, Ulic
Qel-Droma succumbs to the pull of the dark side."* Two Dark Lords now exist who have never
met, and when Kun arrives to stake his claim they fight —

> but stop when **ancient Sith spirits appear and declare them both Dark Lords of the Sith,
> master and apprentice.** Many Jedi flock to the newly reborn Sith order, forming a
> hierarchy of lesser Sith Lords.

**Note the recruitment.** *Many Jedi flock.* The Order did not merely lose a war; it lost
members, to a cause that had just been publicly endorsed by the dead.

### 3,996 BBY — the Mandalorians change the arithmetic

The Krath empire expands out of the Deep Core, and immediately hits the problem every
conquering cult hits: *"The Krath's ability to control the planets they have conquered is
strained."*

**It is solved by a duel.** On **Kuar**, Ulic Qel-Droma defeats **Mandalore the
Indomitable** in single combat — and under Mandalorian custom that binds the clans to the
victor. *"With the Mandalorians bound to the Sith Lords, their combined forces flow outward
from the Deep Core and begin conquering planets across the galaxy."*

**This is the hinge of the entire war**, and the Campaign Guide says so directly: Ulic
*"uses their prowess and numbers to redouble the Sith war effort. With both the Krath and
the Mandalorians at their command, the Sith ravage the galaxy and deal the Republic blow
after blow."* A cult with a fleet became a cult with an army.

Meanwhile *"as more and more Sith train on the planet Korriban, thousands of Jedi die at
the hands of Krath and Mandalorian forces."*

**Coruscant.** *"After months of conquering planets from the Deep Core to the Outer Rim,
the Sith launch their attack against the capital planet of the Republic."* The attack
fails in one specific respect: **Ulic is captured and put on trial for treason.**

### 3,996 BBY — the collapse

The war ends in the same year it peaked, in a run of disasters that fall mostly on the Sith
themselves.

**The Cron Cluster.** *"Aleema Keto, leading the forces of the Krath in battle near the
Cron Cluster, is killed after activating a supernova-inducing weapon aboard her warship.
The Krath space fleet is devastated."* One of the two people who started all of this died
by her own weapon, and took her side's navy with her.

**Onderon.** *"After an intense battle at Onderon, Mandalore the Indomitable is killed and
the Mandalorians suffer a stunning defeat."* The army Ulic won at Kuar is broken, and the
Mandalore who lost that duel dies in the wreck of the bargain.

**Ossus, and a brother.** The Campaign Guide's narrative gives the moment the war actually
turns, and it is not a battle:

> The tide turns once more in favor of the Republic as the brothers **Ulic and Cay
> Qel-Droma** engage in a vicious duel on Ossus. After slaying Cay, a despondent Ulic
> surrenders to the Jedi **Nomi Sunrider.**

**Ulic killed his brother and stopped.** Everything the Republic could not achieve with
fleets was achieved by a man putting down his weapon.

**Yavin 4.** *"Exar Kun and the remnants of the Sith are defeated at Yavin 4. The Great
Sith War comes to an end when Republic forces liberate Empress Teta."*

### What the victory actually bought

**Less than it looked like.** The Campaign Guide is unsentimental:

> Following his capture, the Republic begins an offensive that drives the forces of the
> Sith back, eventually shattering the Krath forces, routing the Mandalorians, and
> scattering the few remaining Sith to the farthest reaches of the galaxy. **Crippled by
> the prolonged war effort, the Republic cannot pursue the Sith forces, and Korriban
> remains under Sith control.**

**The Republic won the war and could not afford to finish it.** Korriban — the world that
trained Exar Kun, the world Adas united twenty-four thousand years earlier — stayed in Sith
hands. So did the survivors: *"The Sith who had fled back to Korriban bide their time and
keep a low profile, while those who had fled to the Unknown Regions practice their dark
arts in secret."*

And the Mandalorians were routed, not destroyed. They *"continue their nomadic existence,
rebuilding their ranks and continuing the Mandalorian tradition… marshal their forces in
secret for a return to the galactic stage."*

**Both of the next two wars in this book were already loaded here.**

What follows immediately is not a war but a recovery — the **Restoration Period,
3,995–3,966 BBY** — thirty years in which the Republic rebuilt its trade routes and its
military while *"the Mandalorians marshal their forces in secret for a return to the
galactic stage."* **Those thirty years open Chapter Four**, since they are that war's
run-up rather than this one's aftermath.

## Major Figures

*Short entries. Each points back into the narrative above rather than re-telling it.*

**Exar Kun** — Jedi apprentice who left his master for Sith lore and was made Dark Lord on
Korriban; co-elevated with Ulic Qel-Droma by the Sith spirits (*see 3,997 BBY above*).
Defeated at Yavin 4 in 3,996 BBY, which ends the war. **Kun's Korriban training is the
direct link between this era and Chapter Two's deep history** — the tradition the exiles
founded was still teaching.

**Ulic Qel-Droma** — the war's tragedy and its hinge. Went into the Krath as an
infiltrator and fell (*see 3,997 BBY*); won the Mandalorians at Kuar, which is what made the
Sith a galactic threat rather than a Deep Core one (*see 3,996 BBY*); captured at Coruscant
and tried for treason; killed his brother Cay on Ossus and surrendered to Nomi Sunrider,
which is where the Campaign Guide places the turn.

**Satal and Aleema Keto** — the Empress Teta aristocrats who found the Sith material and
founded the Krath in 4,000 BBY (*see above*). Aleema died at the Cron Cluster by her own
supernova weapon in 3,996 BBY, destroying the Krath fleet.

**Mandalore the Indomitable** — lost the duel at Kuar that bound the clans to Ulic
Qel-Droma, and was killed at Onderon in 3,996 BBY (*both above*). **The office outlives
him**; his successor opens Chapter Four.

**Nomi Sunrider** — the Jedi to whom Ulic surrendered on Ossus. Named by the Campaign
Guide's narrative at f.5; she does not appear in its dated timeline.

**Cay Qel-Droma** — Ulic's brother, killed by him on Ossus. The death that ended Ulic's
war.

**Marka Ragnos** — **⚠ the Campaign Guide contradicts itself about him, and this chapter
does not hide it.** Its narrative account names his spirit as the tempter of *both* Jedi.
Its own dated timeline says otherwise — Kun and Ulic fall by separate routes, and the pair
are elevated by *"ancient Sith spirits"*, plural and unnamed. **This chapter follows the
timeline**, which is the more precise of the two: dated, itemised, and event by event
against a five-paragraph summary.

**Either way he is an *ancient* Sith Lord**, dead long before any of this. His relevance is
posthumous, which is the era's characteristic horror in one figure.

## Sources for this chapter

**All of it is the *Knights of the Old Republic Campaign Guide*** — rank 2 throughout, and
the page numbers are the printed ones. **No lower-ranked source was needed anywhere in this
chapter.**

- **"The Great Sith War" and "The Restoration Period"**, the narrative accounts — p. 5
- **The dated timeline**, 4,000 BBY through 3,950 BBY — pp. 112–113

**⚠ Where those two disagree, this chapter follows the timeline**, on the principle that
the more precise account wins: a dated, itemised list beats a summary compressing four
years into five paragraphs. **Both readings are reported rather than one quietly dropped**
— the Marka Ragnos entry above is the one place it matters.

**Where they do not disagree but only one has the material, both are used.** The duel on
Ossus, Cay Qel-Droma's death and Nomi Sunrider's name appear in the narrative and not in
the timeline; the timeline supplies the years the narrative leaves out. **The two are
complementary far more often than they conflict.**
