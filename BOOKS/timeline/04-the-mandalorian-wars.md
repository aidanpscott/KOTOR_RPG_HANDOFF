# Chapter Four — The Mandalorian Wars

**Status: DRAFT, for review.** Opens with the Restoration Period as its first movement,
per MAIN's ruling — not every Campaign Guide era needs its own chapter, and a connective
buildup belongs folded into what it sets up. `PT-1802`'s three parts throughout.

---

**This is the war that makes the campaign possible.** It produces Revan, it produces Malak,
it produces the fleet that comes back wearing different colours, and it produces the
particular kind of broken Mandalorian that a player meets on Taris in 3,956 BBY.

It is also the war the Jedi Order refused to fight, and **that refusal is the hinge** —
not the Mandalorians, not Malachor V. Everything that goes wrong afterward starts with a
Council saying no and a young Jedi deciding the answer was wrong.

---

## The events

### The Restoration Period, 3,995–3,966 BBY — thirty years of rebuilding, and a rearmament nobody saw

**The Great Sith War did more damage than anything in a thousand years.** The Campaign
Guide measures it against the only comparable disaster: it *"causes more damage than any
conflict since the Great Hyperspace War a thousand years before."*

The accounting is grim and worth reading in full, because it explains everything the
Republic does next:

> The Jedi Order, greatly depleted, turns inward to heal itself, increasing the
> responsibilities of a Republic that is barely standing. Piracy is rampant. Trade routes,
> which had been blazed and developed over centuries at enormous cost, require
> redevelopment at nearly every stop. **Nearly one quarter of the civilized worlds in the
> Republic have been devastated by the conflict**, and most worlds are left on their own to
> rebuild as the Republic's resources are depleted.

**The Republic was on the verge of collapse for over a decade.** What saved it was not a
fleet or a Jedi — it was a deal. *"A group of politicians put into motion a series of
events that eventually restores the Republic to its prior strength,"* by *"guaranteeing the
various corporations throughout the galaxy safe passage and trade along the space lanes in
exchange for commercial investment in the Republic's infrastructure."*

That bargain has a date attached: **3,985 BBY, the Coruscant Financial Exchange
Establishment act**, which *"facilitates the restoration of the galaxy by allowing galactic
corporations to invest in the Republic's infrastructure."*

**It worked.** The military was rebuilt, goods reached devastated worlds, hyperspace
explorers went out again *"discovering newer and safer routes,"* and commerce resumed.

**And two enemies used exactly the same thirty years.**

> The Sith who had fled back to Korriban bide their time and keep a low profile, while
> those who had fled to the Unknown Regions practice their dark arts in secret. The
> Mandalorians continue their nomadic existence, rebuilding their ranks and continuing the
> Mandalorian tradition. Though the Sith would remain silent for a time, **the Mandalorians
> marshal their forces in secret for a return to the galactic stage.**

**The Republic rebuilt in public and its enemies rebuilt in private, on the same clock.**
That is the whole Restoration Period in one sentence, and it is why this era opens the
chapter rather than closing the last one.

### 3,976 BBY — the raids begin, and a new order is founded

*"Under the leadership of **Mandalore the Ultimate**, remnants of the Mandalorian clans
begin testing the Republic's defenses by raiding Outer Rim worlds."*

**⚠ This is not the Mandalorian Wars, and the distinction is ruled** (`PT-855`). It is
twelve years of probing — raiding and *"interrupting trade routes, forcing the Republic to
push its resources further than they were prepared for."* A war starts later and elsewhere.

**This is also when the Neo-Crusaders are founded**, and by whom matters: *"an order of
Mandalorians sponsored by **Mandalore the Ultimate** following the end of the Great Sith
War."* `PT-675` exists because the founding is commonly told backwards — **the Preserver
did not found them.** The Preserver *regrouped* them in 3,951 BBY, fifteen years after they
had ceased to exist.

**The Mandalorians of this chapter are therefore not the Mandalorians of Chapter Three.**
Those were the Crusaders, bound to Ulic Qel-Droma at Kuar and broken at Onderon. **This is
a second order, founded from the wreckage of the first**, by a Mandalore who watched what
happened last time.

### 3,973 BBY — Cathar

*"**Cassus Fett** leads the Mandalorian massacre on Cathar. Few Cathar survive the assault,
but **the Republic suppresses news of the attack**, fearing it might destabilize
reconstruction efforts."*

**Hold onto the second half of that sentence.** The Republic knew, and buried it, because
the reconstruction was fragile and the truth was inconvenient. **That decision detonates
ten years later**, in a way the people who made it could not have predicted.

### 3,965 and 3,964 BBY — probing becomes invasion

*"The Republic engages the Mandalorians in small proxy skirmishes along the Outer Rim"* in
**3,965 BBY** — the first direct contact between the two militaries.

Then, in **3,964 BBY**: *"The Mandalorians stage a massive invasion of Republic space,
**beginning the Mandalorian Wars.**"*

**The pattern was deliberate.** *"When the Republic has been stretched thin across the
Outer Rim, the Mandalorians launch a major offensive, invading worlds and devastating the
Republic military in battle after battle,"* striking *"the Republic's poorly defended
targets."* Twelve years of raiding had not been harassment; it had been **reconnaissance
and dispersal**, and the invasion arrived at the moment the Republic was thinnest.

*"Chaos spreads throughout a Republic that fears the devastation of the Great Sith War."*
They had good reason to fear it. Most of them had lived through it.

### 3,963 BBY — the Council says no

*"After a series of military failures, the Republic once again turns to the Jedi Order for
aid."*

**The Order refused**, and the Campaign Guide gives its reason without editorial: *"Having
been hit the hardest by the casualties of the Great Sith War, the Jedi Order refuses, at
first, to join the military effort."* The dated timeline puts it plainer still — the
Council *"refuses, claiming that their losses during the Great Sith War were too great."*

**This is the most consequential sentence in the chapter.** It is not cowardice and it is
not folly. Chapter Three is the evidence for the Council's position: thousands of Jedi
dead, an Order that had to turn inward for thirty years to survive at all, and a war that
began when Jedi went looking for dark-side knowledge and found it.

**Revan went anyway.**

> Among the Jedi are a few who cannot stand by while the Mandalorian invasion batters at
> the Republic's defenses, including a powerful Jedi called **Revan.** Revan leads other
> like-minded Jedi into battle once more, **despite the Order's directive that they should
> not be involved.** After victories against the Mandalorians, even more Jedi flock to
> Revan's call.

And **3,973 BBY comes back**. In the same year the Council refuses, *"Revan discovers and
reveals the truth about the massacre at Cathar, winning dozens of converts from the Jedi
Order."* The Republic's ten-year-old decision to bury Cathar becomes the recruiting
argument that splits the Jedi. **These Jedi are branded the Revanchists**, and the
Mandalorians overrun Duro, Iridonia, Serroco and Taris while the argument runs.

*"Over time, the Republic cedes control of many of its military assets to the Jedi, and the
Mandalorian Wars begin in full."* **The Republic handed its war to a faction of its own
religious order that was acting against that order's instructions.** Nobody in this book
ever quite reckons with what that meant.

### 3,961 BBY — two discoveries, and only one of them is about the war

*"Revan discovers the Rakatan star map at Kashyyyk."* Chapter Two's Infinite Empire stops
being history at this exact moment.

*"Revan travels to Malachor V and discovers the Trayus Academy, **delving deeply into the
Sith secrets contained within.**"*

**The war is being won and something else is being lost**, in the same year, by the same
person.

### 3,960 BBY — Malachor V

*"Revan lures the Mandalorians to Malachor V, where a battle ensues between the Republic and
the Mandalorians. Revan defeats Mandalore the Ultimate, activates the superweapon known as
the **Mass Shadow Generator**, and shatters the Mandalorian fleet."*

The Campaign Guide's narrative calls it *"a devastating victory"* after which *"the
Mandalorian forces are nearly wiped out"* and *"the surviving Mandalorians go into
exile."* `EVENTS-01` is more exact about the terms: **Mandalore the Ultimate is slain, the
surrender is unconditional, and the Mandalorians are stripped of arms and scattered.**

**The Neo-Crusaders end here**, four years after the war they were rebuilt to fight began.

And then: *"the Jedi are heralded as saviors of the Republic. **Surprisingly, though, Revan
and Malak gather the remnants of their fleets and flee into the Unknown Regions.**"*

> For a short few months, peace reigns in the Republic once more, but **questions surround
> the departure of the Jedi.**

That is where this chapter stops and Chapter Five starts.

### What is left, and what the campaign actually meets

**The Mandalorians do not reappear as a power for nine years**, and the campaign sits
inside that gap. `EVENTS-01` divides them into three states, not two:

| Era | Years | Under |
|---|---|---|
| Crusaders | ~7,000 – 3,996 BBY | ends at the Great Sith War (Chapter Three) |
| Neo-Crusaders | 3,964 – 3,960 BBY | Mandalore the Ultimate |
| **The gap** | **3,960 – 3,951 BBY** | **scattered — no faction. Our setting is inside it** |
| Reformed | 3,951 BBY onward | Mandalore the Preserver, on Dxun |

**⚠ But "no faction" is not "no Mandalorians," and the corpus corrected itself on exactly
this point** (`PT-681`). At 3,956 BBY they are *"dispersed mercenaries with no command and
no territory"* — and already re-organising. Without the **Mask** there is no Mandalore, so
*"the various clans fought amongst themselves for power"* while *"most of the warriors soon
became mercenaries or bounty hunters."*

**The search for the Mask is what puts them back together.** Clan Ordo began looking; *"less
than fifty Mandalorians were part of the search on the first world, taking them two years,
but as they moved from planet to planet, their numbers grew as more clans joined them… by
the time they started searching Rekkiad in 3,954 BBY, over a hundred clans had joined."*

> **The correct statement is: clans without a Mandalore. Not an absence — a fragmentation
> with a cause already pulling it back together.**

**And they are visible in KOTOR 1 itself**: Canderous Ordo, *"the most famous Mandalorian
mercenary in the Taris sector,"* working as Davik Kang's enforcer; Sherruk's band troubling
the settlers of Dantooine; six mercenaries stranded on Taris. **Every one of them is a
consequence of Malachor V**, and a GM who plays them as a standing army has the era wrong.

## Major Figures

*Short entries. Each points back into the narrative above rather than re-telling it.*

**Mandalore the Ultimate** — founded the Neo-Crusaders in 3,976 BBY from the wreckage of
the Crusaders (*see above*), spent twelve years probing before invading in 3,964, and was
slain by Revan at Malachor V in 3,960. **The one Mandalore in this book who planned a war
rather than answering one**, and the last until the Preserver.

**Revan** — refused the Council's refusal (*see 3,963 BBY*), revealed Cathar, led the
Revanchists, won the war at Malachor V, and vanished into the Unknown Regions with the
fleet. **Also the person who found the Trayus Academy in 3,961** — Chapter Five is the
consequence of that, not of the war. The Rakatan star map at Kashyyyk in the same year is
Chapter Two's Infinite Empire re-entering the story.

**Malak** — Revan's apprentice; *"Jedi such as Revan and his apprentice, Malak, become war
heroes,"* and left with him in 3,960. Chapter Five is his.

**Cassus Fett** — led the massacre on Cathar in 3,973 BBY (*see above*). **The Republic's
suppression of his atrocity, not the atrocity itself, is what splits the Jedi ten years
later.**

**Canderous Ordo** — a Neo-Crusader who outlived the order. At 3,956 BBY a hired gun on
Taris, which is exactly where KOTOR 1 finds him. **He becomes Mandalore the Preserver and
reunites the clans on Dxun in 3,951** — Chapter Six's business, and the end of the gap this
chapter leaves open.

**Mandalore the Indomitable** — Chapter Three's, not this chapter's, and the distinction
matters: he led the *Crusaders*, lost the duel at Kuar, and died at Onderon in 3,996 BBY.
**A different order under a different Mandalore in a different war.**

## Open items, carried from review

**⚠ Flag 1 — the Campaign Guide's era heading and its own timeline disagree on when the
war starts, and `EVENTS-01` has already ruled it.** The era section is headed **"The
Mandalorian Wars (3,965–3,960 BBY)"**; the dated timeline puts *"a massive invasion of
Republic space, beginning the Mandalorian Wars"* at **3,964 BBY**, with 3,965 carrying only
*"small proxy skirmishes."* Same source, same rank, neither superseding — so `PT-946`
applies and the dated, itemised timeline is the more specific reading. **`EVENTS-01`
reached the same answer independently and recorded it as `PT-855`, citing the timeline
verbatim at rank 2.** This chapter follows 3,964 and records the heading. **No new ruling
needed** — noting it because it is the second same-rank conflict in two chapters, both
between a CG summary and the CG timeline, which now looks like a pattern rather than a
coincidence.

**⚠⚠ Flag 2 — `EVENTS-01` cites Campaign Guide *line numbers* under a `p.` label, and the
corpus also uses `p.` for printed folios.** `EVENTS-01` cites *"CG p.10187"*, *"p.10195"*
and *"p.10198"* for the 3,976, 3,965 and 3,964 entries. **Those are OCR line numbers in
`KOTOR-CG-OCR.txt`, not pages** — verified: line 10187 is the 3,976 raiding entry, 10195
the proxy skirmishes, 10198 the invasion. Meanwhile `D-W42` cites *"Timeline p.113"*, which
**is** a printed folio. **So `p.NNN` now means two different things in the corpus**, exactly
the shape of the rank-7 collision `PT-1800` just fixed. A human tells them apart by
magnitude — a 200-page book has no p.10187 — but an automated sweep will not.
**Recommend a distinct prefix for OCR line cites; not mine to rule.**

**⚠⚠ Flag 3 — a superseded claim is still standing as an assertive heading in `EVENTS-01`,
and it is `PT-961`'s exact shape.** `§2` records that `PT-675` said *"no faction,"* that
**`PT-681` corrected this as "too strong,"** and that the true position is *"clans without
a Mandalore… not an absence."* **But further down the same section, under an empty heading
reading `### ⚠ The superseded claim`, sits a live-looking heading: `### ⚠⚠ AND THAT MEANS
THERE IS NO MANDALORIAN FACTION AT 3956 BBY`.**

**Exact lines, so this is actionable:** `EVENTS-01:45` carries the correction (*"`PT-675`
said 'NO FACTION.' That was too strong"*); **`:69` is the empty `The superseded claim`
heading**; **`:71` is the superseded claim itself**, set as a level-3 heading with no
marker that it is dead.

The empty heading was evidently meant to label what follows as superseded, but **as the
document renders, the superseded claim reads as a current conclusion** — and it directly
contradicts the correction twenty-six lines above it. This is `PT-961` precisely: *"when a
ruling changes a table, it does not change the sentences that read from it."* **A reader
skimming headings gets the retracted answer.**

**This chapter follows `PT-681`**, the later and correcting ruling. The practical difference
is real: "no faction" would license playing 3,956 BBY as *no Mandalorians*, when the correct
reading is dispersed mercenaries with a reunification already under way. **Wants fixing in
`EVENTS-01`; not mine to edit.**

**⚠ Flag 4 — a smaller internal wobble in `EVENTS-01`'s Neo-Crusader dates.** `§1` and `§2`
both put the founding at **3,976 BBY**; `§3`'s era table gives the Neo-Crusaders as
**3,964–3,960 BBY**. Not a contradiction if the table means *the years they fought the
Wars*, which is the natural reading — but the table is labelled as an era, and it is the
row a sweep would read. This chapter states both: **founded 3,976, fought 3,964–3,960,
disbanded 3,960.**

**Recorded so nobody re-adds it: `PT-855` also removed the Battle of Althir** from the
3,976 entry. It is not in the Campaign Guide and is not in this chapter.
