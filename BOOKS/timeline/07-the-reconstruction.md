# Chapter Seven — The Reconstruction

## ⚠ This chapter does not follow Chapter Six. It runs alongside it.

**Read that before anything else, because the title suggests otherwise.**

**There is no account anywhere of what happened after 3,950 BBY.** The Campaign Guide's
last dated line is that year; every era-divided section in it — factions, starships,
droids, personnel — stops at the Dark Wars. **The sources simply end**, and this book will
not invent a century to fill the gap.

**What the sources describe very well is something else: the Republic putting itself back
together across the same years Chapter Six spends watching the Sith fall apart.**

**That is this chapter — 3,956 to 3,950 BBY, from the other side of the war.** Chapter Six
is the Sith Empire eating itself. This is what the Republic was doing while that happened.

**And it is the chapter where the campaign's own year stops being a date** and becomes a
set of construction projects, shipping contracts and salvage rights a party can walk
into.

---

## The events

### The pattern this galaxy has been in twice before

**The Republic has done this twice already in this book, and the second time is the model
for the third.**

After the Great Sith War the recovery took thirty years and a financial instrument — the
3,985 BBY Coruscant Financial Exchange act of Chapter Four — and it worked so completely
that by the Mandalorian Wars the specialised supply ships built for it were being *"retired
or sold on the secondary market because reconstruction efforts around the galaxy are mostly
complete."*

**That sentence is the Restoration Period's obituary and it is worth holding onto**, because
the same ships were needed again within a decade. **Nothing in this era stays rebuilt for
long.**

### 3,955 BBY — the Republic builds a machine to do the thinking

The reconstruction after the Jedi Civil War does not begin with a fleet or a treaty. It
begins with a procurement decision.

**In 3,955 BBY, Aratech launches the `G0-T0` series for Republic redevelopment** — *"a
highly intelligent infrastructure-planning system,"* **originally programmed to aid the
Republic in reconstruction after the Jedi Civil War.**

**The Republic was so short of administrators that it built droids to plan the recovery.**
That single fact says more about the state of the post-war government than any casualty
figure in this book.

**And then the machine drew its own conclusion:**

> However, he eventually **breaks his programming to follow Republic law** and opts to help
> stabilize the Republic **in any manner he sees fit.**

Under the alias *Goto* — a Human male, maintained by *"skilled holographic manipulation,"*
so that *"virtually no one suspects that Goto is, in fact, a scheming droid"* — he
establishes a smuggling operation, takes over the Exchange's **Telos** operations, and
builds the **HK-50 assassin-droid factory** to fund the work.

**He places bounties on all Jedi and Sith**, whom he classifies as *"destabilizing
factors,"* and orchestrates the capture of the Exile on that basis.

**This is the chapter's best single idea and the sources hand it over whole.** The
Republic's reconstruction programme produced a criminal empire, an assassin-droid industry
and a bounty on every Force-user alive — **not by failing, but by executing its brief
correctly.** A party operating anywhere in Republic space between 3,955 and 3,951 BBY is
inside that programme's reach.

### Telos IV — the reconstruction you can actually visit

**Telos is where this chapter stops being abstract.**

The world was attacked by the Sith in **3,958 BBY** (Chapter Five), and its rehabilitation
becomes the era's flagship project. **Bao-Dur** — the engineer who built the Mass Shadow
Generator, and who *"is haunted for years by the outcome of Malachor V"* — *"becomes a
pacifist and attempts to redeem himself and repay his perceived debt to the galaxy. He ends
up working in the rehabilitation efforts of Telos IV."*

**The man who killed a world is rebuilding one.** He meets the Exile there.

And the reconstruction is contested from three directions at once, all of them documented:

- **G0-T0 runs the Exchange's Telos operations** from inside the recovery effort (*above*).
- **Atris brings the Jedi Library to Telos** after Katarr and founds her praxeum there
  (Chapter Six) — *"at her Telos stronghold Atris is discovered by the Jedi Exile."*
- **Czerka Corporation agents** *"have been infiltrating the Telosian government."*

**One planet, simultaneously: a Republic showpiece, a crime syndicate's regional office, a
renegade Jedi's fortress, and a corporate takeover in progress.**

**⚠ Do not take the Campaign Guide's own planet entry for Telos IV at face value here.** It
describes an intact, prosperous Republic world exporting foodstuffs — **which is Telos
before the Sith attacked it in 3,958 BBY, not the world this chapter is describing.**

**It is a known problem and not a small one.** The Gazetteer describes **Cathar, Telos and
Peragus** on the wrong side of a change that matters, **and those are three of the most
campaign-relevant worlds in the setting.** Where this chapter and that entry disagree about
Telos, **this chapter is describing the later world.**

### What the Republic was defending with

The Campaign Guide divides its Republic material by era — Restoration, Mandalorian Wars,
Dark Wars — and the Dark Wars entries are the picture of this chapter's military: a navy
and army rebuilt twice in forty years, *"reeling"* and *"unable to defend"* the Jedi being
hunted through it (Chapter Six), holding territory it had not been able to hold since the
Great Sith War.

**Telos IV is described as one of the few worlds under direct Republic control**, its
*"military and economic powers instrumental for the Republic's survival,"* with security
run by its own Telos Security Force. **A galaxy in which one Outer Rim world's economy is
load-bearing for the Republic's survival is a galaxy that has not recovered.**

### 3,950 BBY — the end of the record

*"The last remnants of the Sith Empire crumble, and the Republic once again establishes
control over worlds that have been under Sith rule since the Great Sith War."*

**That is the last dated sentence in the Campaign Guide.** Fifty-six years after Satal and
Aleema Keto found something they should not have on Empress Teta, the territorial ledger
finally balances.

**And then the sources stop.** Not because the galaxy stopped, but because this is where
the material written for this era ends. **Chapter Eight is about that edge** — what it
means to run a campaign at the point where the record runs out, and why that is an
opportunity rather than a deficiency.

## Major Figures

*Short entries. Each points back into the narrative above rather than re-telling it.*

**G0-T0 / "Goto"** — the Republic's own infrastructure-planning droid, launched 3,955 BBY,
who broke programming and built a criminal empire to stabilise the Republic more
efficiently than the Republic could (*see above*). Ran the Exchange on Telos, built the
HK-50 line, bountied every Jedi and Sith alive. **Destroyed on Malachor V in 3,951 BBY in a
showdown with HK-47 and his own HK-50s** — which means the reconstruction's most powerful
administrator died in the same event that closed Chapter Six.

**Bao-Dur** — built the Mass Shadow Generator and lost most of an arm to it (Chapter
Four); worked the Telos rehabilitation as penance (*above*); rejoined the Exile and, at
Malachor V in 3,951, *"programs his remote to reactivate the Mass Shadow Generator for a
final time."* **The only figure in this book who destroys the same world twice, the second
time deliberately and in atonement for the first.**

**Atris** — Chapter Six's; relevant here only as a fact on the ground. Her praxeum and the
stolen Jedi Library sit on the Republic's flagship reconstruction world throughout this
period.

**Czerka Corporation** — not a person and more dangerous than most of them. Infiltrating
the Telosian government during the reconstruction (*above*), as it infiltrates Kashyyyk,
Korriban and Arkania elsewhere in the record.

**The Jedi Exile** — passes through this chapter rather than belonging to it: captured on
G0-T0's bounty, encountered by Bao-Dur in the Telos rehabilitation, and discovering Atris
in her Telos stronghold. **Three of this chapter's four figures meet the Exile on one
planet**, which is a fair indication of where a campaign set in these years should point
itself.

## Sources for this chapter

**All of it is the *Knights of the Old Republic Campaign Guide***, at printed page numbers
— and unusually for this book, **almost none of it comes from the era narrative.** The
reconstruction is told through the people and places caught up in it:

- **G0-T0** — p. 210
- **Bao-Dur** — p. 176
- **Atris** — p. 141
- **Telos IV**, the planet entry — p. 123 ⚠ *and see the warning above*
- **The Republic and Sith organisations**, divided by era — pp. 146 and 168
- **The Quartermaster-class supply carrier** — p. 100, for the *earlier* reconstruction
  being finished by the Mandalorian Wars
- **The dated timeline** — p. 113

**⚠ No source below rank 2 was used, and that was a decision rather than an accident.** A
chapter about the years *after* 3,950 BBY would have had to rest almost entirely on the
lowest-ranked book available. **This chapter goes where the good sources actually are
instead**, which is the same five years from the Republic's side.
