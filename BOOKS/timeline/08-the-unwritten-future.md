# Chapter Eight — The Unwritten Future

> *"Look, everybody always figures the time they live in is the most epic, most important
> age to end all ages. But tyrants and heroes rise and fall, and historians sort out the
> pieces."*
>
> — **Jolee Bindo**, quoted in the KOTOR Campaign Guide, f. 6

---

## Where the record stops

**Chapter Seven ends on the last dated sentence anyone wrote for this era:**

> *3,950 BBY: The last remnants of the Sith Empire crumble, and the Republic once again
> establishes control over worlds that have been under Sith rule since the Great Sith War.*

**After that, nothing.** Not sparse coverage — **nothing.** No source describes a single
dated event later than 3,950 BBY, and every part of the Campaign Guide that divides itself
by era — factions, starships, droids, personnel — stops at the Dark Wars.

**This chapter is about that edge**, and the first thing to say about it is the most
important:

**The sources stopped. The galaxy did not.**

Those are different facts and this book has spent seven chapters being careful not to
confuse them. A world that no source describes is not a world that ceased to exist. **The
record ends in 3,950 BBY for the same reason it is dense in 3,956: somebody wrote a game
about one and not the other.**

## What is known, and how little it constrains you

**In one sense the far future of this galaxy is the most thoroughly documented fiction in
the world.** Everyone at the table knows how it goes: a Republic that lasts, then decays,
then falls; an Order that grows large and complacent and is murdered almost to the last;
an Empire, a rebellion, a family.

**In the sense that matters here, none of it is relevant.** Chapter One sorted that
material into its own category — *canon but irrelevant* — for a reason worth restating at
the end of the book:

**They have not happened yet. They will not happen for four thousand years.**

**To measure it properly, compare it against this book.** Everything in Chapters Three
through Seven — the Great Sith War, the Restoration, the Mandalorian Wars, the Jedi Civil
War, the Dark Wars, the whole arc five chapters are spent on — **happens inside fifty
years.** The gap between the last page of that and the rise of the Empire is **nearly four
thousand.**

Put another way: Palpatine is about as far from a player character in 3,956 BBY as
Hammurabi's Babylon is from the reader.

**Nothing in this book's future is foreshadowed by anything in its present**, and a
Gamemaster who plants Skywalker-era seeds is not enriching the era — they are borrowing
from a story four millennia away that has no causal connection to this one. **The galaxy's
long arc is known in outline and inert in practice.**

## The threads the sources leave hanging on purpose

**What the record actually hands forward is a small number of deliberately open questions**,
and they are open in the way a door is open rather than the way a wound is.

**Revan is gone.** *"Revan leaves once more for the Unknown Regions."* The most consequential
person in four chapters walks off the edge of the map and the sources close behind them.

**The Exile's quest ends and its consequences do not.** The Jedi Order is a handful of
survivors beginning to rebuild. What they rebuild it into is not written.

**Atris is on Telos** with the Jedi Library and a stack of Sith holocrons, running a
praxeum she built to shape the Order to her taste.

**The Mandalorians have a Mandalore again.** Canderous Ordo reunited the clans on Dxun in
3,951, and the Preserver era opens with no stated end.

**The Star Forge's fate is a branch, not a fact.** As Chapter Five said, it is **the only
event in this book whose outcome your campaign decides** — who destroyed it is settled at
your table rather than in any source.

**And KOTOR 3 was left open deliberately.** Chapter One's four categories include one
reserved for **what has not been written** — and KOTOR 3 is the only thing in it. **That is
not an oversight. It is a slot held empty on purpose.**

## The rule for filling the gaps

**The rule is nine words long, and it is the same source ladder from Chapter One pointed
at the act of making things up:**

> **Invent freely in the gaps; never contradict a ranked source.**

**One principle, deliberately permissive**, in place of a list of what you may and may not
touch.

**Read both halves.** *Invent freely* is the larger half. Everything this book has not
said is yours: the worlds nobody surveyed, the people nobody wrote down, what happened on
the far side of the Outer Rim while Revan was busy. **Seven chapters of constraint exist to
make the eighth chapter safe, not to make it small.**

*Never contradict a ranked source* is the whole of the limit. Not *never surprise the
players*, not *never deviate from the games* — **never contradict something the record
actually says.** The record says less than most people assume, which is exactly why this
book spent so much effort establishing what it does say.

### The one question this book will not answer

**Most questions a player asks fall into one of three kinds, and only the third is hard:**

| The question | What to do |
|---|---|
| The book answers it | Read it off the page |
| The book is silent, and nothing depends on the answer | **Invent freely** |
| The book is silent, but something in play depends on it | **That is the Gamemaster's call, not a lookup** |

**The middle row is where this chapter lives.** The third is small — and its most famous
member is the one this book has been circling since Chapter Five.

**What happened to Revan?**

**This book does not know, and neither do the sources.** The Campaign Guide's last word is
*"Revan leaves once more for the Unknown Regions."* KOTOR 2 makes the search for Revan a
thread running through an entire game **and still does not say.**

> **The silence is not an omission. It is the shape of the story.**

**So this chapter will not tell you either** — not out of coyness, but because answering it
would mean inventing and calling it reporting, **which is the one thing this book has
refused to do for seven chapters.**

**That question belongs to your table, and the record was built to leave it there.**

## Why the edge is the best part

**A campaign set in 3,956 BBY sits four years before the last thing anyone wrote down.**

That is an unusual and enviable position. **Run a game in most well-documented settings and
the players are tourists** — every consequential thing has already been decided by someone
else, and the table's job is to visit it. **This era runs out almost immediately.** Within
five years of the default clock, the sources are silent, and everything after that is the
table's.

**And what the record does say is unusually generous to a Gamemaster.** It leaves a
reunified Mandalorian nation with no stated future, a Jedi Order small enough to be
remade by half a dozen people, a Sith tradition twenty-four thousand years old that has
survived every previous extinction, a Star Forge whose fate the table decides, and a galaxy
full of Rakatan machinery still buried where the Infinite Empire dropped it — with the
Campaign Guide itself calling that last one *"a blank slate."*

**None of that is a gap in the record. It is the shape the record was given.**

## What this book was, one last time

**Chapter One said this book is not the authority on dates, and that stands.** The sources
hold the record; this book explains it. **Where the two disagree, the sources are right and
these pages have a mistake in them.**

**What this book claimed to do was smaller and, I hope, more useful:** to say what is
actually known about the long history behind this campaign, to say who says it and
how much weight they carry, to record where the sources disagree rather than quietly
picking a winner, and — here at the end — **to be equally clear about where the knowing
stops.**

Everything from 4,000 BBY to 3,950 BBY in these chapters is someone else's, faithfully
reported and precisely cited.

**Everything after 3,950 BBY is yours.** Tyrants and heroes rise and fall. The historians
have sorted out the pieces up to here, and then they stop, and the rest of it is for the
people at the table.
