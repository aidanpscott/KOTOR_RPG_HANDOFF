# FORCE-AWAKENING-01 — Force Sensitivity, and how a character finds it

**All three stages settled.**

**Replaces:** `Force-Sensitive` as a feat bought with GM permission.
**Depends on:** `INFLUENCE-01` (the accrual shape and the anti-farming construction), `MULTICLASS-01 §3.3` (the gate this feeds).
**Source posture:** authored. **KOTOR 1 and 2 are evidence of design intent; AD&D 1e psionics is prior art on shape, not on values.**

---

## 1. Why this is authored rather than ported

**RCR gives `Force-Sensitive` as a feat. A character buys it and the GM permits it.**

**That makes becoming a Jedi a bookkeeping event.**

> **In both games it is a discovery.** **Revan dreams. The Exile sees potential in others and trains them.**

**The mechanic should produce that story rather than sit beside it.**

**⚠ And the design has to work at a table with no software.** **If the rule only runs with an app, it is not a tabletop rule.**

---

## 2. Three stages

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage 1 — The Echo** | Something happens to the character that they did not choose. Dice decide whether it fires and what it means. **Nothing is told to the player** |
| **Stage 2 — The hidden determination** | Two tags are set: *is this character sensitive*, and *can they Echo again*. **Neither is revealed.** *The tags exist for the engine and the GM'''s notes* |
| **Stage 3 — Confirmation** | A Force-user tells them what they are. **Only now is the feat available** |

**⚠ The three-stage shape is what makes the fiction and the mechanic the same thing.** **The character experiences strangeness, then certainty that something is wrong, then an explanation from outside.**

---

# STAGE 1 — THE ECHO EVENT

## 3. What an Echo is

**A moment where the Force acts on a character who does not know it exists.**

**Not a power. Not a choice. Something that happens *to* them.**

> **⚠ The character experiences it as strange, unsettling, or lucky. Never as evidence.**

**That is what keeps Stage 3 necessary.**

---

## 4. The manifestation list

**Handbook content, for a GM or an AI to draw from. Organised by tier, then by family. Open — extend freely.**

**⚠ Each Echo is drawn from a tier no lower than the last.** **The character's own reading of themselves escalates with the tier.**

### 4.1 Tier 1 — Deniable

> ***"That was lucky."***

**Precognitive** — You look at a door a moment before it opens. · You pick the other corridor for no reason and the first one was a dead end. · You wake a second before the alarm. · You stop walking and the crate falls where you would have been.

**Perceptual** — You know someone is lying and cannot say how. · A room feels wrong and you leave. · You pick the right one of three identical crates. · You recognise a stranger's mood before they speak.

**Kinesis** — A dropped cup lands upright. · A door you did not quite reach swings the rest of the way. · The one loose bolt you needed is already in your hand.

**Resonance** — A ruin presses on you. · You sleep badly in one specific room. · A crystal is warm when nothing else is.

**Bleed** — A crowd's tension arrives before the fight does. · You feel sick before you learn why.

**Survival** — A fall you walk away from. · The one time the aim was off. · The charge that did not detonate.

**⚠ Every Tier 1 entry has a mundane explanation and the character will reach for it.**

### 4.2 Tier 2 — Uncomfortable

> ***"That keeps happening."***

**Precognitive** — The dream repeats, with more detail each time. · You move before the shot and only realise after. · You brace before the ship is hit. · You say a name a second before someone else does.

**Perceptual** — A presence in an empty room. · You know how many people are behind a door. · You know which of them is armed. · Someone's grief reaches you across a corridor.

**Kinesis** — A weapon you are reaching for closes the last few centimetres. · Something rattles when you are angry. · A light flickers when you do.

**Resonance** — An object you cannot put down. · A place you have to leave and cannot explain why. · You dream about a world you have never been to and describe it correctly.

**Bleed** — Someone else's fear becomes yours mid-sentence. · You take on a companion's mood without noticing. · A dying stranger's last feeling lands in you.

**Survival** — Twice now. · You come out of a firefight unmarked and cannot account for it. · A poison that should have worked.

**The pattern is the point.** **Any one of these alone is Tier 1; happening again is what makes it Tier 2.**

### 4.3 Tier 3 — Undeniable to the character

> ***"Something is wrong with me."***

**Precognitive** — You dream a death and it happens. · You know the ambush is there and there was no way to know. · You finish a sentence someone has not started.

**Perceptual** — You feel someone die light-years away. · You know a companion is lying about something they have never mentioned. · You sense a Force-user in the room before you see them.

**Kinesis** — Something moves that you did not touch, in front of witnesses. · A weapon leaves someone's hand. · You catch what you could not have reached. · Something breaks when you are angry.

**Resonance** — You stand where a massacre happened and see it. · An object shows you its history. · A place refuses you.

**Bleed** — You cannot tell which feelings are yours. · A crowd's panic moves through you and you move with it.

**Survival** — Something should have killed you and simply did not, and everyone saw.

**⚠ The character now knows. They still do not know *what*.**

## 5. When Echoes fire — four layers

### 5.1 Layer 1 — The campaign package

**A package declares which moments are eligible, and may close the list entirely.**

| Posture | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Any qualifying moment is eligible. The GM or AI proposes freely |
| **Guided** | The package names some events and permits others |
| **Closed** | **The package names every eligible moment. Nothing else fires, ever** |

**⚠ Closed exists for tightly-authored packages.** **A specific story with specific beats should be able to say *"the awakening happens here, or not at all."***

**⚠ Campaign packages are not designed yet.** **Two questions are owed to that workstream: the posture field, and how much latitude an AI has to invent eligible moments from setting, timeline and milestones.**

### 5.2 Layer 2 — The GM or AI proposes

**Reading the setting, the timeline, what just happened.**

**⚠ The AI proposes. It does not decide.** **`intent → resolve → narrate`, as everywhere else.**

### 5.3 Layer 3 — The uncontrolled test

> **Would the character have avoided this moment if they could?**

**If yes, it qualifies. If no, it does not.**

**Walking into a fight is a choice — the fight does not qualify.** **The blaster bolt that should have hit you does.**

### What does not qualify

**Four confirmed exclusions. None of them dead-ends — each already does something else.**

| Does not produce an Echo | Because it already does this |
|---|---|
| **Meditating** | **Restores the Force pool** — `FORCE-POOL-01-v3`, `REST-AND-MEDITATION-01` |
| **Asking a Force-user to test you** | **That is the cold test** — `§13`. A `d100` roll that can close the door |
| **Anything you can repeat** | *The test, restated* |
| **Anything you can schedule** | *The test, restated* |

> **⚠ The last two are the whole rule in four words.** **Repeatable and schedulable are what *controlled* means operationally, and a GM can check them faster than the counterfactual.**

### ⚠ The one exception — Force-strong sites

**Visiting a nexus, a tomb, a grove or a battlefield DOES qualify, even though going there is a choice.**

**Because the alternative contradicts the manifestation list.** **`Resonance` is a family at all three tiers** — *a ruin that presses on you, a place you have to leave, standing where a massacre happened and seeing it.* **Excluding sites would delete a sixth of `§4`.**

**And the fiction is right: a place with the Force in it acts on you whether you meant it to or not.**

**⚠ Whether a given site can produce an Echo is the GM's or the AI's call. The assumption is yes.**

**A site qualifies unless the GM says otherwise.** **They do not have to justify a no** — *some ruins are just ruins, and some tombs have been picked clean of more than treasure.*

> **Assumed yes, overridable to no, and the override needs no reason.**

**⚠ Which means a player can never be sure a site will do anything, and that is the point.** **A site the party sought out specifically because they hoped for an Echo may simply be quiet.**

**⚠ Bounded so it is not farmable:**

> **Once per site, ever. Per character.**

**The first time a place presses on you is the moment. Going back does not do it again — you know what it feels like now.**

**⚠ And the number of such sites in a campaign is set by the package and the Atlas, not by the player.** **A player cannot manufacture a Korriban tomb.**

**⚠ This is the entire anti-farming construction.** **To farm Echoes a player must repeatedly put their character somewhere they would rather not be, at real risk.** **A player willing to do that has earned them.**

**No cap per session. No cooldown. The test does the work.**

**Two modes, and a GM picks:**

**Judged** — the GM applies the test. **Default. Fast, and it puts Echoes where the story is.**
**Rolled** — the GM does not judge. At the end of each scene involving real risk, roll `d20`. **On 18+ the scene qualifies, then roll normally for the Echo.** *Slightly rarer by construction, which is correct — a GM not filtering for meaning lets more scenes through, so the extra gate compensates.*

### 5.4 Layer 4 — Rarity

**Not every qualifying moment produces an Echo. See `§6`.**

## 6. Roll 1 — does an Echo fire?

| Echo | Fires on | Chance |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | **19+** | 10% |
| **Second** | **17+** | 20% |
| **Third** | **15+** | 30% |
| **Fourth** | **13+** | 40% |
| **Fifth on** | **11+** | 50% |

> **One `d20` at a qualifying moment. The target drops by 2 for each Echo already had.**

**At least one Echo: 41% after 5 qualifying moments · 65% after 10 · 88% after 20.**

---

## 7. Roll 2 — is the character Force-sensitive?

**The flavour dial. This number says how common Force-sensitivity is in the galaxy.**

**Rolled at each Echo, for a character not yet determined.**

| Echoes | Character level | Confirms on | Chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — cold test** | **1–4** | **96+ on `d100`** | 5% |
| | **5–9** | **98+** | 3% |
| | **10–14** | **99+** | 2% |
| | **15–19** | **100** | 1% |
| | **20+** | **no roll** | **0%** |
| **1** | any | **18+ on `d20`** | 15% |
| **2** | any | **15+** | 30% |
| **3** | any | **12+** | 45% |
| **4** | any | **9+** | 60% |
| **5 on** | any | **6+** | 75% |

**⚠ Row zero is the cold test and it uses `d100`, not `d20`.** **A `d20` runs out of headroom by level 5; the cold test needs room to scale across thirty levels.**

> **The fiction: the older you are, the more set you are.** **A young person who has never felt anything might still be waiting to. A veteran of twenty levels who has never once felt the Force almost certainly never will.**

**⚠ Which makes the dilemma sharper over time.** **The cold test is not merely bad — it gets worse while the player is deciding.**

### The Wisdom trigger

**When a character's Wisdom rises above its previous highest, an `Open` character is *armed*.**

> **⚠ Armed, not triggered. The next Echo fires it.**

**When it fires: one free re-check of Roll 2, at the current Echo's row.** **Then it is spent.**

**Why it is arranged this way:**

**An ability increase arrives on a fixed schedule and a player knows exactly which level it lands on.** **⚠ An immediate re-check would fire on that schedule, which is what `§5.3` exists to prevent.**

**Requiring an Echo to resolve it means half the trigger is yours and half is the galaxy's.**

> **Growing wiser does not hand you an answer. It means that the next time something happens, you are readier to understand it.**

**⚠ A character who arms it and never has another Echo never gets the benefit.** **Correct, and the same shape as `Closed` — the system offers, the galaxy decides.**

**⚠ `Mysticism` is a Wisdom skill.** **Wisdom is the attribute this whole document runs on; Charisma reaches the design at `FORCE-TRAINING-01 §2`'s `Persuade` gate. Each touches the system once.**

**⚠ Escalation on the `d20` rows is steep because the cap is three.** **A character who Echoes three times should feel like the answer is coming.**

**⚠ An Echo firing does not mean the character is sensitive. It means something happened.**

---

## 8. The cap

**Default: three Echoes without confirmation. Then they stop.**

**⚠ After the cap, no further Echoes fire until a Force-user confirms what the character is.** **The story has to move.**

**Campaign-package tunable.** *Short package: 2. Long slow-burn: 5. A package that wants the mystery unresolved: 1.*

# STAGE 2 — THE HIDDEN DETERMINATION

## 9. What happens at an Echo

**Roll 2 fires. Then one of two things.**

**Sensitive.** **Tag set. Nothing told to the player.** **The character keeps Echoing to the cap, and the answer sits waiting for Stage 3.**

**Not sensitive.** **Roll 3 asks whether the Force will try again.**

**⚠ The tags exist for the engine and the GM's notes. They are never surfaced to the player.**

## 10. Roll 3 — the mercy dial

**Only after a failed Roll 2.**

| | On `d20` | Chance |
|---|---|---|
| **`Open` — the question stays live** | **6+** | **75%** |
| **`Closed` — permanently not** | 1–5 | 25% |

**⚠ Failure ends a character's possibility forever. That should be uncommon.**

**Roll 2 says how common Force-sensitivity is. Roll 3 says how cruel the system is about closing doors.** **Tune them separately.**

**Cumulative, with these numbers:** **15% sensitive after one Echo, 34% after two, 49% after three.**

## 11. The six states

| Tag | Means | Player knows |
|---|---|---|
| **`Undetermined`** | No Echo yet | — |
| **`Open`** | Not sensitive, question live | **No** |
| **`Sensitive`** | Determined, awaiting confirmation | **No** |
| **`Closed`** | Permanently not | **No** |
| **`Confirmed — yes`** | A Force-user has told them | **Yes** |
| **`Confirmed — no`** | A Force-user has told them | **Yes** |

> **⚠ `Open` and `Closed` must look identical from the outside. Both see nothing.**

## 12. What `Closed` looks like at the table

**The Echoes simply stop. Nothing is said.**

**⚠ The moment a GM narrates *"you never feel it again,"* the player knows** — and the whole point is that nobody knows until a Force-user says so.

**A `Closed` character and an `Open` character should both be able to hope.**

---

## 13. The cold test

> **A character may ask a Force-user to test them without waiting.**

**Failure closes the door permanently, in every state.**

### 13.1 What the roll is, by state

| State | The test |
|---|---|
| **`Undetermined`** | **Row zero of `§7`, on `d100`, scaled by level** |
| **`Open`** | **Their Echo-count row of `§7`, on `d20`** |
| **`Closed`** | **No roll. The Force-user says no** |
| **`Sensitive`** | **No roll. This is Stage 3 — the answer already exists and is being revealed** |

**⚠ An `Open` character tests at their Echo row, not row zero.** **They have had Echoes; there is more to read.**

**But the gamble is the same gamble.** *A player with two Echoes can force a 30% test now, or wait for a third at 45%. Still worth waiting — but it is a real choice rather than an obvious one.*

### 13.2 Why it is different in kind, not merely worse

**With Echoes, sensitivity was already determined the moment the first one fired.** **The Force-user is *revealing* an answer that exists.**

**Without Echoes, nothing has been determined.** **The test itself is the determination, and it is a bad one.**

> **⚠ You are forcing an answer out of a universe that has not given you one, and forcing it is how you get the wrong one.**

### 13.3 The incentive, priced

    cold test           5%    and failure is permanent
    after  5 moments    7%
    after 10 moments   13%
    after 20 moments   20%
    after 50 moments   25%

**Waiting beats testing cold at every point past the very start, and the gap widens.**

**⚠ The impatient choice is the one that closes the door.**

---

## 14. What the whole pipeline produces

| Qualifying moments | Force-sensitive |
|---|---|
| **10** | **13%** |
| **20** | **21%** |
| **50** | **25%** |

**Roughly one in five adventurers who survives a full campaign of genuinely dangerous moments turns out to be Force-sensitive.**

**⚠ Against AD&D 1e wild talent: ~1% of characters, decided once, at creation, forever.**

> **The difference is not the rate. It is that our dice control *pace*, never *possibility* — except at a determination point, which can close the door but can never close it by attrition.**

## 15. Who does what

| Layer | Owner | What |
|---|---|---|
| **The rolls, the tags, the cap** | **Code** | Deterministic, auditable |
| **Whether an event matches the schema** | **Code** | Not a judgement call |
| **Noticing the moment** | **AI or GM** | Proposing, not deciding |
| **The manifestation** | **AI or GM** | The dream, the intuition, the wrongness |

**⚠ The GM does exactly what the AI does.** **Every number in this document is a plain `d20` target and the first roll that matters is a single die against 19.**

# STAGE 3 — CONFIRMATION

## 17. Who can tell you

| Who | How |
|---|---|
| **Jedi Masters, Sith Masters, trained Jedi and Sith** | **Automatic. No roll** |
| **Force adepts, sages, non-aligned practitioners** | **Automatic. No roll** |
| **Padawans and Sith apprentices** | **Mysticism check. ⚠ Once ever, per target** |
| **A confirmed Force-sensitive PC** | **Mysticism check. ⚠ Once ever, per target** |
| **Untrained sensitives** | **Cannot** |

**DCs, on `SKILL-RESOLUTION-01`'s ladder:**

| Reading someone | DC |
|---|---|
| **They have had Echoes** | **20 — Hard** |
| **They have had none** | **25 — Formidable** |

**⚠ Same DC for a padawan and a PC.** **Their skill ranks already say who is better; a second thumb on the scale double-counts it.**

## 18. A failed reading is a lead, not a dead end

**A padawan or apprentice who fails does not shrug.** **They send you up the chain** — to their master, to the enclave, to the Council.

> **⚠ And when everyone reachable has failed, that is the mission.** **Get to Coruscant. Get to Dantooine.**

**That should be a real journey and not a side errand.** **It is also the answer to *"what if there is no Jedi"* — there is no backstop rule, because the absence is the story.**

## 19. Self-suspicion — what a high-Mysticism character gets alone

**A character who experiences an Echo may sense that *this was a Force thing*.** **`Mysticism`, DC 15 — Moderate.**

| Total bonus | Needs | Chance | Auto on take 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **+0** | 15+ | 30% | no |
| **+2** | 13+ | 40% | no |
| **+5** | 10+ | 55% | **yes** |
| **+8** | 7+ | 70% | **yes** |
| **+10** | 5+ | 80% | **yes** |
| **+14** | — | **auto** | **yes** |

> **⚠ It never confirms anything.** **A `+14` Mysticism character auto-succeeds at *"that was a Force thing"* and still does not know whether *they* are the reason.**

**Plenty of people stand near the Force without being it.** **A character can suspect for ten sessions and be wrong.**

## 20. What confirmation costs

**The telling is free. Getting to the teller is not.**

**A Jedi or Sith will simply tell you — sometimes unprompted, if they notice.**

**But: travel to a temple or enclave. A fare. Waiting, because they are busy and you are not a priority.** **⚠ Access, not payment.**

## 21. The feat

> **The feat is granted secretly at Stage 2 determination. It is hidden. `Confirmed — yes` reveals it.**

**It costs nothing. The awakening was the payment.**

**⚠ App design requirement, recorded here because the chargen schema does not exist yet:** **the engine holds a granted-but-hidden feat, and the `Confirmed — yes` tag flips its visibility.**

> **The character sheet carries state the player cannot see.** **That is `E-11` territory — *no sheet field is reliably cosmetic* — and the chargen schema needs to represent it when it is written.**

## 22. What Stage 3 does NOT cover

**⚠ Confirmation is not training.**

**A character can walk out of a temple knowing exactly what they are, with nobody willing to teach them.** **That is Jolee. That is half of KOTOR 2.**

> **The Jedi classes are gated by finding a teacher, not by knowing what you are.**

**`FORCE-TRAINING-01` owns that: the Persuade gate, the age ladder, `Marked by the Academy`, the Council, and the class unlocks.**

---

## 23. Open

**Nothing in the three stages. Two notes forward.**

**⚠ Force-strong sites want mechanical weight beyond Echoes.** **`§5.3` gives them one qualifying moment per site per character and nothing else.** **When the Atlas is written, a nexus should probably do more — and it should be something other than an Echo.**

**⚠ Age bands for sixteen species.** **`FORCE-TRAINING-01 §6` owns it.** *Fourteen are an extraction job; Kaleesh and Echani must be authored.*

**Two items closed rather than carried:**

**Attribute influence on Roll 2 — declined.** > **`Mysticism` is a Wisdom skill — `SKILLS-01`, derived, not recalled.** **Wisdom already reaches every roll in this document that a character makes about themselves.** **Applying it a second time to Roll 2 would double-count.** **`§14`'s figures stand.**

**⚠ And Charisma reaches the design elsewhere** — **the `Persuade` gate in `FORCE-TRAINING-01 §2`.** **Each attribute touches the system once, in the place it belongs.**

**⚠ `MULTICLASS-01 §3.3`'s dead warrant — resolved.** **`FORCE-TRAINING-01` is the warrant, not this document.** **The gate is about who may take a Force class, and that is training rather than awakening.** **`FORCE-AWAKENING-01` answers *what am I*.**
