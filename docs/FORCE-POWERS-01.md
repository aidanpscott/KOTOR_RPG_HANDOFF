# FORCE-POWERS-01 — What Each Power Does

**Source: `Force_Powers_Table.docx`, 88 powers.** `source_system: kotor_game`, secondary.
**Costs, tiers, and degradation joined from `POWER-COSTS-01`, which governs on mechanical values.**

> **This document existed and was never sent to the playtesters.** **Two S4 reports independently found that `POWER-COSTS-01` supplies cost and no effect** — one authored eleven powers to run the scenario, the other refused and ran Force casts as bookkeeping only. **Both were right to flag it.**

**`PARTITION-01 §6.6`: where this and the 2DA disagree, the 2DA governs on mechanical values and this supplies prose.**

## ⚠⚠ HOW FAR A PARTY-WIDE POWER REACHES — `PT-2274`, `PT-2275`, AND THIRTEEN OF THEM ARE AUTHORED

**⚠⚠ `PT-2274` RULES THE BINARY: a power either targets one individual, or it
is a genuine area effect, and the geometry itself does the limiting.** Nothing
artificial sits on top — no cap, no manual multi-select. **Area is the norm
for a party-wide power**, and a buff especially.

**⚠ SO A PARTY-WIDE POWER WITH NO RADIUS HAS NOTHING LIMITING IT.** Measured:
of the seventeen powers with `affects: ally`, **sixteen are area** and one —
`Revitalize` — states its own selection rule outright (*"the single closest
fallen ally"*) and needs none.

    stated, and read           `Heal`                                    7
    stated, and NOT read       `Improved Heal` · `Master Heal`           7
    stated nowhere             the other thirteen                        —

**⚠⚠ THE MIDDLE ROW WAS ONE PREPOSITION — `PT-2275`.** `Heal` says *"all party
members **within** a 14 metres — 7 squares radius"*; its own two higher tiers
say *"**in** a 14 metres — 7 squares radius"*. Same sentence, same geometry,
and every reach pattern required `within`. **Two thirds of the `Heal` chain
had no reach at all.** Fixed in the reader, where the fault was — the document
was never wrong.

**⚠⚠ AND THE THIRTEEN ARE AUTHORED, WHICH IS WHY EACH ROW SAYS SO.** Their
prose states *"all party members"*, *"the entire party"*, *"every party
member"* — who, and no distance. `PT-2275` rules **`Heal`'s own 7 squares as
the default**, so the one figure the corpus already states for a party-wide
power governs the rest rather than thirteen separate inventions. Each row
carries `⚠ AUTHORED REACH` so a reader never mistakes it for a transcription,
which is the same discipline `Force Choke`'s three rounds and `EQUIPMENT-01`'s
armour grades are held to.

**⚠ THE CHAIN THAT SPLITS IS WORTH SEEING.** `Revitalize` is *"the single
closest fallen ally"* and its `Improved` and `Master` tiers are *"all
allies"* — one tier single-target, two tiers area, inside one chain. This is
not a per-chain decision and a reader should not treat it as one.

## ⚠⚠ HOW A PRINTED DURATION BECOMES ROUNDS — `PT-24`, AND THE CORPUS HAS TWO CONVERSIONS

**A Force power's printed seconds are divided by THREE.** That is `PT-24`'s own rule, and its heading states it outright: *"Force power durations convert at printed seconds ÷ 3."*

**⚠⚠ AND AN ITEM'S ARE DIVIDED BY SIX, WHICH IS NOT THE SAME RULE AND IS NOT A CONTRADICTION.** `PT-1859` ruled the item clock at six seconds to the round — `ACTION-ECONOMY-01`'s own figure — and said in the same breath that `PT-24` does **not** reach items, because applying it to one doubles the real clock: that is how a 120-second adrenal became forty rounds. Two conversions, each scoped, and the scope is the whole of the distinction. A power is balanced against the pool it costs; an item is a stopwatch.

**⚠⚠⚠ ONE TABLE IN THE LEDGER CROSSES THAT LINE, AND IT IS THE ONE THAT IS WRONG — `PT-2305`.** `PT-255`'s *"Durations, converted at 6 seconds a round"* lists eleven FORCE POWERS and converts them at the item rate, without citing `PT-24` or noticing it exists. It disagrees with this document about every single power it names, and **the disagreement is unanimous in this document's favour** — measured across every power that carries a figure:

| power | printed | `PT-255`'s table | `PT-24` ÷ 3 | what ships |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fear | 6 s | 1 | **2** | 2 |
| Force Stun | 9 s | 2 | **3** | 3 |
| Horror | 12 s | 2 | **4** | 4 |
| Force Stasis | 12 s | 2 | **4** | 4 |
| Insanity | 18 s | 3 | **6** | 6 |
| Force Aura | 20 s | 3 | **6** | 6 |
| Burst of Speed | 36 s | 6 | **12** | 12 |
| Breath Control | 240 s | 40 | **80** | 80 |

**Eight of eight.** Nothing was ever built from `PT-255`'s figures; the table is dead data that reads like a rule. **The shipped numbers are correct and it is the table that needs withdrawing** — and that is the owner's file to change, not this one's.

**⚠ AND `PT-24`'s ENTRY SAYS ITS CONTENT IS RECOVERABLE FROM THIS DOCUMENT'S OWN CITATION — which had gone.** The ruling's body was lost in the `C`-series rename and its register entry points here to recover it; no citation of `PT-24` remained in this file, so the rule survived only in the heading of an entry that says to look somewhere else. It is cited here now, which is what makes that pointer true again.

## ⚠⚠⚠ A TIER REPLACES THE ONE BELOW IT — `PT-2304`, EXTENDED BY `PT-2336`

**`PT-2304` ruled it from the source's own sentence** — *"Knight Valor replaces
the bonus granted by Force Valor"* — **and two chains said so while the rest of
the corpus said nothing.** The `Force Valor` and `Force Camouflage` chains
carried `replaces:`; the `Force Scream`, `Force Choke`, `Crush Opposition I`,
`Force Slow`, `Battle Meditation`, `Inspire Followers I` and `Force Aura`
chains did not.

**⚠⚠⚠ THE SILENCE WAS NOT A DECISION, AND IT WAS COSTING.** Measured in a live
cast before it was ruled: **`Force Scream` cast twice left twelve rows on one
trooper** — six from each cast — and the reader that folds them SUMS, so `−2`
became `−4` on every ability score. **Three `Master Force Scream` casts reach
`−18` to all six.** Not a rounding disagreement; a stacking exploit.

> **⚠⚠ RULED: the same *replaces, not stacks* principle applies universally.**
>
> *"There's no principled reason a debuff or damage-penalty chain should be
> allowed to stack indefinitely when the exact same tiered structure, applied
> to a buff, was already ruled to replace — if anything, unconstrained
> penalty-stacking is the more severe failure mode … A power should not
> restack a penalty on recast any more than it restacks a bonus."*

**⚠⚠ THE MODIFIER CHAINS ONLY *LOOKED* SAFE, and that is worth stating because
it is the reason nobody noticed.** `Force Shield` over a standing `Force Aura`
left both rows in place; the reader for those rows happens to take the best and
the worst rather than the sum, so the number came out right. **Right by
accident, from a fold that was never meant to be a tier rule.** `Force Valor`
is the proof: its two halves agree only because the displacement runs before
either of them.

**⚠ SO IT IS ASKED OF EVERY CHAIN THAT GRANTS A STANDING ROW, and the
extractor refuses one that stays silent** — a new tier authored tomorrow would
stack exactly the same way, and nothing downstream could tell.

**⚠⚠ AND IT IS ASKED ONLY WHERE THERE IS SOMETHING TO REPLACE.** A prerequisite
that grants no standing row has no bonus to supersede: `Force Choke` follows
`Force Strangle`, which grants none, so naming it would be a claim about a row
that does not exist. **Recasting a power on ITSELF is not a `replaces` question
at all** — the reader already displaces what a power's own id put there, which
is why `Force Choke` needs no entry and still cannot stack on itself.

## ⚠⚠ THE KINDS A POWER MAY BE AIMED AT — `PT-2235`, AND THE SET IS CLOSED

> **⚠ THERE ARE THREE: `sentient` · `beast` · `droid`.**

**⚠⚠ BOTH HALVES OF THE TARGETING GATE ALREADY USED THIS SET AND NEITHER DECLARED IT.** `kindOf` returns `droid`, `sentient` or nothing; the `targets:` annotations below use `sentient`, `beast` and `droid` and nothing else. **A vocabulary the code assumes and no document states** — the same shape `affects` had until `PT-2229`.

**⚠⚠ AND DECLARING IT IS WHAT LETS A NEGATIVE MEAN SOMETHING.** `excludes` is transcribed from prose — *"this power does not affect droids"*, said twenty-five times — and until the set is closed it can only REFUSE. A power excluding droids, aimed at a sentient, fell through to *"does not say what it may be aimed at"* and the gate stayed silent. **With three kinds and one excluded, the other two are stated.** Twenty powers move from silent to judged on this line alone.

    excludes: droid          →  may be aimed at  sentient · beast
    excludes: beast · droid  →  may be aimed at  sentient

**⚠ AND THE DISTINCTION `PT-1486` DREW SURVIVES IT.** *"A list that omits a kind is not the same as prose that excludes it"* — so a completed negative still refuses in the negative's own words, *"does not affect a droid"*, rather than borrowing the positive's *"is aimed at sentient · beast, not a droid"*. **What the reader sees still says which half refused.**

**⚠⚠ `beast` IS IN THE SET THOUGH NOTHING CAN CURRENTLY BE ONE — ruled, `PT-2235`.** `kindOf` can never return it: *"`beast` exists in the `targets` vocabulary and nothing in a package is one yet."* **Leaving it out would quietly make *excludes droid* mean *sentient only***, which is a different claim from the one the prose makes, and it would be wrong the day a beast is placed.

**⚠⚠ AND `self` IS NOT ONE OF THEM — `PT-2235`.** It was in the `targets` column on one power and it is not a kind of thing, it is who a power is AIMED at; `affects` has answered that question since `PT-2229`. The copy in the wrong column was not merely redundant: `Dark Healing` read `targets: self`, so the gate answered *"Dark Healing is aimed at self, not a sentient"* for **every real kind** and the power could not be cast at all. `TEST 095` reported that refusal verbatim.

### The Strangle chain, rebuilt on dice — `PT-260`

**⚠ `Force Kill` was the odd one out.** **`Force Strangle` and `Force Choke` are damage-over-time; `Kill` was a percentage of the target's *maximum* vitality.**

> **⚠ Which meant it hit for the same 100 against a full-health Soldier and against one with 30 points left.** **Used twice it killed anything.**

**The opposite of `PT-177`'s asymptote, which is the property that made `Assassin Protocols` acceptable.**

| **Force Choke** | **`1d6` per 4 Force levels**, and the target is `Stunned` | 2 | 14 | 35 | 49 |

| Tier | Save fails | Save passes |
|---|---|---|
| **Force Strangle** | `1d6` per 5 FL, 2 rounds, `Stunned` | **⚠ nothing** |
| **Force Choke** | `1d6` per 4 FL, 2 rounds, `Stunned` | **`1 ×` Force levels once, no stun** |
| **Force Kill** | `1d6` per 3 FL, 3 rounds, `Stunned` | **1d6 per Force level to 20, then 1d6 per two levels — maximum 25d6** — `PT-301` — `PT-258` |

**⚠ There is no attack roll.** **Force powers in this system are save-or-suffer; the points are spent either way.**

**KOTOR gives the consolation damage to `Kill` alone** — *"they are not choked, but instead suffer damage equal to attacker level"* — **and says of `Wound` and `Choke` that a successful save *"results in no effect."***

> **Extended to `Choke`. Not to `Strangle` — owner ruling.**

**⚠ And it scales — `PT-263`.** **`PT-261` gave `Choke` and `Kill` the same `2 ×` Force levels, which is flat across a chain whose whole point is escalation.**

    Force Strangle   nothing
    Force Choke      1x Force levels     FL10 = 10 · FL20 = 20 · FL30 = 30
    Force Kill       2x Force levels     FL10 = 20 · FL20 = 40 · FL30 = 60

**⚠ `1 ×` is the source's own figure for `Kill`** — *"damage equal to attacker level."* **`Kill` doubles it because it is two tiers up.**

**⚠ Which makes the tier-1 entry the gamble and the upper two the reliable ones.** **A tier-1 power that always does something would make the chain's own progression flatter, and the entry tier is where a whiff should cost you.**

#### ⚠ `Stunned` applies at all three tiers, on a failed save only — `PT-262`

**`PT-261`'s table dropped the stun from `Force Strangle`.** **`PT-260` had recorded it as one of the four places our version deliberately diverges from the source, and the table written one ruling later did not carry it.**

> **⚠ The correction reached the ruling and stopped at the table. Third instance — `PT-240` and `PT-244` were the first two.**

**All three stun. None stuns on a successful save.**

**⚠ Which means the tiers separate on dice and duration alone:**

    Strangle   1d6 per 5 FL, 2 rounds, no consolation
    Choke      1d6 per 4 FL, 2 rounds, 2x FL on a save
    Kill       1d6 per 3 FL, 3 rounds, 2x FL on a save

**That is a cleaner progression than one where the condition arrives late** — **the chain does one thing harder each tier rather than acquiring a new verb, which is `PT-182`'s test.**

#### ⚠ Derived from the source's own arithmetic

**KOTOR's `2/3 attacker level every **1 round** for **2 rounds**` is three ticks across two KOTOR rounds** — **`1 ×` level per round.**

**`1d6` per 4 Force levels averages exactly that at level 20.** **The tiers move the divisor rather than inventing a curve.**

#### What it fixes

**⚠ Damage scales with the caster's power, not the target's health bar.** **A dying enemy is no longer worth 100 damage.**

**And it cannot one-shot.** **`105` at Force level 30 is `1.3 ×` a Barrage across three rounds — during which the target acts twice.**

**⚠ The save clause changes from `100 vs 20` to `105 vs 60`.** **The power stays worth casting when the save succeeds, which it was not before.**

#### ⚠ And it holds the owner's progression rule

> **Each tier rolls more dice; `Kill` runs one round longer.** **Extend what came before rather than replacing it.**

**`Force Strangle` keeps the stun our version added and KOTOR's `Wound` lacks** — **owner ruling, `PT-260`: our versions stand where they diverge.**

## What joined and what did not

**88 powers with descriptions. 79 matched a cost row in `POWER-COSTS-01`.**

**Where the cost column reads `—` the power is in the description table and not in the priced roster** — **`PARTITION-01` cut twelve powers to move `Crush Opposition` and `Inspire Followers` into feats, and the rosters have not been reconciled since.**

**`Restricted By Armor` in the Requires column matters:** **`EQUIPMENT-01 §5.2` records that armour blocks Force powers, and this names which ones.**
