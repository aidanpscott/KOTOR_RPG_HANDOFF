# FINDINGS-37 — the multiclass rule, ruled

**Owner-approved. Two lines, and they close the last item standing between here and a complete prestige tier.**

---

# 1 — The rule

> **A character holding more than one class uses the **highest attack-pick rate** among them, and the **highest chain count** among them.**

**Both apply from the level of entry forward. Picks already spent are not recalculated** — `CLASS-ATTACKS-01 §5`, unchanged.

**⚠ The rate half supersedes `FINDINGS-36 §1`'s prestige-only floor.** **It was scoped to prestige because that was the case in front of us; the owner has extended it to any multiclass character and the wording is simpler for it.**

**⚠ And it is an exception to `MULTICLASS-01 §3.1`, which rejected take-higher by name — *"neither summing nor taking the higher rate."*** **That section needs amending rather than reading around.**

---

# 2 — Why highest, and it is arithmetic rather than preference

**Your rate sets `T`, the total tiers you can place. A chain absorbs 1 to 3. `N` trees absorb between `N` and `3N`.**

> **⚠ Taking the *lower* chain count can leave picks with nothing legal to buy.**

    Consular 10 / Weaponmaster 20   ->   rate Combat, T = 40

    Consular's       N=13    3N=39    strands 1 pick
    Weaponmaster's   N=15    3N=45    strands nothing

**Tested across every band overlap: the higher count never strands, at any rate, in any pairing. The lower one sometimes does — up to 16 tiers in the extreme case.**

**`CLASS-ATTACKS-01 §2.3` already rules what that costs:** *"A stranded pick is worse than a wasted one. Three currencies, no crossover — it cannot become a feat or a skill point."*

## 2.1 And it is not a free upgrade, which is what makes it safe

**Capstones are `⌊(T − N) ⁄ 2⌋`. A higher chain count means **fewer** finished chains.**

    Consular 10 / Weaponmaster 20    N=13 -> 13 capstones    N=15 -> 12 capstones

**The multiclass character opens more trees and completes fewer.**

> **⚠ Which is what the rulebook already promises.** **`MULTICLASS-01`, player-facing:** *"A specialist is better at their thing. A multiclass character has more things."*

**The rule makes the arithmetic say the sentence that is already printed for players to read. That is the strongest argument available for it and it required no new machinery.**

## 2.2 Cost at the table

**Two comparisons. Highest rate, highest chain count.** **No lookup table, no weighting, no per-pairing entry.**

**⚠ Compare what was rejected on the way here:** **`MULTICLASS-01 §5`'s four deleted entry-credit systems, one of which was *"grants shaped by target class and tier distance — correct, and it took seven steps at the table to answer what do I get for switching."*** **This is two steps and no state on the sheet.**

---

# 3 — What this closes

**`FINDINGS-35 §2`** — the Battlemaster's rate boundary. **Closed: no boundary lowers a rate any more.**
**`FINDINGS-36 §1.1`** — *"your current rate"* undefined for a multi-base character. **Closed: highest held.**
**`FINDINGS-36 §4`** — which chain count applies to a multiclass character. **Closed: highest held.**

**⚠ And the Guardian trap that started this is gone entirely.** **A Guardian may take any Jedi prestige class and lose nothing, which was not true four exchanges ago and was not visible on any sheet.**

---

# 4 — What it does not close

**⚠ The upward gains stand and were accepted deliberately** — `FINDINGS-36 §3`. **A `Specialist` Force class entering the `Weaponmaster` or `Battlemaster` at 10 gains twelve picks and gives up seven Force powers and a sixth of its pool.**

**⚠ And `MULTICLASS-01 §3.1` now contradicts the ruling.** **It states the per-level rule and rejects take-higher. It is not mine to edit; flagging that it is the one document that will read as still governing.**
