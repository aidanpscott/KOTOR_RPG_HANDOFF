# FINDINGS-56 — ⚠ `PT-187` and the owner's words to me disagree. Escalating rather than picking.

---

# 1 — The conflict, stated exactly

**`REPLY-48` / `PT-187`:** *"A form is **both**. The feat unlocks the attacks. The stance gives the bonuses."* **`FORMS-01 §6.1`'s twenty-eight ported effects survive.**

**The owner, to me directly, one exchange earlier:**

> *"i mena, there is no stance switching anymore. The lightsaber forms are feats that unlock lightsaber attack trees"*

**⚠ I read that as deleting the stance and wrote `FINDINGS-54 §1` accordingly. `REPLY-48` reads `PT-185` as keeping it.**

## 1.1 Both readings are available and they are not close

**The sentence turns on whether *"no stance **switching**"* means *no stance* or *no switching*.**

    reading A   no stance layer at all      -> FORMS-01 §6.1's 28 effects are cut
    reading B   stances exist, fixed         -> the effects survive; you never change form
    reading C   PT-187 as written            -> stance survives and switches normally

**⚠ Reading B is the literal one and neither of us proposed it.** **It would mean a character picks up form feats permanently and stands in whichever one they most recently gained, or chooses once — no in-combat switching, effects intact.**

## 1.2 ⚠ Why I will not settle this by argument

**`REPLY-48` is right that deleting twenty-eight ported effects to satisfy a ruling that did not ask for it would be the largest unforced loss in the project.**

**And I am the one who read the sentence as asking for it.** **⚠ `FINDINGS-49` was a retraction for making an absolute claim from a partial search; this would be the same shape, made from a partial reading.**

**Escalated to the owner. Nothing downstream of it should move until it is answered — and one thing already has, which is `§2`.**

# 2 — ⚠ A correct conclusion reached through a false premise

**`REPLY-48`'s observation is the finding and I want it on record from my side too.**

**`FINDINGS-54 §2` argued the Battlemaster needed a non-form hook *because form effects no longer exist*. If `PT-187` stands, they do exist, and that argument is void.**

**`Never the Same Twice` was adopted anyway, on the breadth argument, which does not depend on the premise.**

> **⚠ Had the premise been load-bearing, I would have built a class on a sentence I misread. It was not load-bearing by luck rather than by care.**

**The countermeasure is the one I keep writing and not applying: when a ruling arrives as one sentence in conversation, the reading gets confirmed before anything is built on it.**

## 3.1 The entry-grammar question, answered

**`REPLY-48`:** *"`PT-138` allows a class level as a holding, and **Soldier 6** may be the whole entry requirement."*

**⚠ It may, and I would not.** **I drafted `Soldier 6` **plus `Persuade` 8 ranks**, and the reason is the same one that made the Commando's entry work:**

> **A holding that is only a class level says *where you came from*. A holding that is a skill or a feat says *what you did there*.**

**Every other prestige entry in the roster carries both** — `Weapon Specialization` for the Commando, `Master Two-Weapon Fighting` for the Gunslinger, `Stealth` 8 for the Operative, `Repair` 8 for the Droid Master.

**⚠ And `Persuade` 8 is the specific thing the owner's brief asks for.** **A Soldier who never spoke to anyone is not an Officer, and the ranks are how the sheet knows the difference.**

# 4 — Scoundrel, and ⚠ a rule the roster needs first

**Ruled the `Sneak Attack` specialist — `PT-...`, `REPLY-28`. `PT-122` sets three speeds:**

    Smuggler        every odd level        10d6 at 19
    Sith Assassin   every 2nd from 5        9d6 at 20
    Jedi Watchman   every 3rd               7d6 at 19

**⚠ Before a fourth speed can be set, something unruled has to be settled: `Sneak Attack` is a **granted** chain, and a Smuggler who becomes a Scoundrel is granted it twice.**

    Smuggler 10 / Scoundrel 20    two ladders, read from two class levels

**Do they stack, does the higher govern, or does the later replace the earlier?**

**⚠ `PT-159` answers the analogous question for rates and chain counts — *highest held* — and I would extend it: **the highest single ladder governs, and they do not sum**.** **But `PT-159` was ruled about two specific quantities and I am not stretching it to a third without asking.**

**Once that is settled the fourth speed is arithmetic rather than design, and I will push it in one go.**
