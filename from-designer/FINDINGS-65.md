# FINDINGS-65 — `Sneak Attack` is a declaration. ⚠ Priced, it is worse than an unconditional chain.

**Owner: *"sneak attack is an action."*** **So `ATTACKS-05 §75` was right and `ATTACKS-01 §223`'s clause is wrong.**

---

# 1 — Applied

> **`Sneak Attack` and `Stealthy Shot` are declarations. You choose one instead of another attack.**

**⚠ `ATTACKS-01 §223` must lose the sentence *"they are not a declaration you choose instead of attacking."*** **It is the framework document, so a reader who checks the governing rule currently gets the wrong answer.**

**⚠ And my `FINDINGS-64 §1.2` recommendation is withdrawn.** **I argued `ATTACKS-01` governed because it is the framework document. That was the right principle applied to the wrong instance — the framework document can be the one that is stale, and here it was.**

---

# 2 — ⚠ Priced, and it does not clear the bar

**One strike at `+6d6` against Korr's three-strike `Barrage`, both at 70% to hit:**

    Barrage, 3 strikes, unconditional            27.3 a round
    Sneak Attack, 1 strike + 6d6                 23.8
    Sneak Attack + Killer's Instinct (+3d6)      31.1

> **⚠ `Sneak Attack` alone is **worse than Barrage** — and Barrage has no condition, fires every round, and does not require you to be hidden.**

**`ACTION-ECONOMY-01 §19.5`: attacking reveals you, so it fires **once per approach**.**

**So a character who spends a tree on it gets, once per fight, something slightly weaker than what another tree gives them every round.**

## 2.1 It only clears the bar with a second chain stacked on it

**`Killer's Instinct` brings it to 31.1 — above Barrage, and that is the number `FEATS-LIBRARY-01` calls *"lethal to most non-boss targets."***

**⚠ But `Killer's Instinct` is granted to three classes and buyable by nobody.** **So the stealth trees are:**

    for those three classes    good, once per approach
    for everyone else          strictly worse than an unconditional chain they already have

**⚠ Which answers the open question in `FINDINGS-64 §4` by arithmetic rather than by preference.** **Leaving the trees open to everyone is safe because nobody outside those classes has a reason to take them.**

**But *safe* is not the same as *right*: an open chain that only one group would ever buy is a restricted chain that has not been labelled.**

## 2.2 What would fix it, if it needs fixing

**⚠ Not mine to rule, and three options in ascending cost:**

**Accept it.** **The chain buys *timing*, not throughput — the first strike of a fight, before anyone acts, against a target who has not moved. `PREGENS-01` shows fights turning on that.**

**Give the top tier a second strike.** **Two strikes at `+6d6` on the first is 40.6, clearly above Barrage, and it makes the tier feel like a capstone rather than a lateral move.**

**Let `Killer's Instinct` be bought.** **⚠ Cheapest in wording and largest in consequence — it is currently the thing that makes three classes special.**

---

# 3 — What the ruling improves

**⚠ `PT-180` no longer applies to it.** **As a declaration it is one strike, so flat riders land once rather than three times. A Sith Warrior's `+6` is `+6` here, not `+18`.**

**`Improved Sneak Attack`'s re-hide clause reads correctly now** — *"you may attempt to re-hide as part of the same declaration"* is coherent for something that **is** a declaration and was awkward for a rider.

**⚠ And the `Scoundrel` gets better.** **`Nowhere To Stand` buys openings, and openings matter more when the whole turn is spent on them — a wasted declaration costs more than a wasted rider. The class I built on conditions rather than dice is the right shape for the ruling that just landed.**
