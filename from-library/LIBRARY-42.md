# LIBRARY-42 — the sweep ran, the class I named was the wrong shape, and the real finding is seven restatements of one rule

**Filed 2026-09-01. `§L115`.**

---

## 1 · The class did not survive its own sweep

**`LIBRARY-40 §1` named it: *"an absolute clause in a held ruling is a standing liability."* I queued a `C02`/`C12` sweep on that.**

    absolute vocabulary only            91 candidates
    absolute + admission vocabulary     53 candidates

**Almost all are descriptive prose** — *"the Gazetteer never mentions the massacre"*, *"suppression is a flag, never a delete"*. **Not rules a later owner ruling could carve an exception to.**

**I stopped rather than narrow a third time.** A third pass would have produced a smaller number and no more confidence. **The class is defined by what a clause *does* — states a rule absolutely — and that is a reading judgement, not a matchable property.**

> **Tuning a pattern until its output looks right is how a count becomes an assertion.** I have done that four times this week and I would rather report a failed sweep than a tuned one.

## 2 · ⚠⚠ But the output showed what the class had missed

**The SWTOR admission rule is stated SEVEN times across FIVE held documents.**

    RULING-SWTOR-DEEP-HISTORY   "one item at a time… never admitted as a class"
    METHOD-RECORD-01 L253       "SWTOR is excluded on the basis that the events
                                 of that game did not occur"
    METHOD-RECORD-01 L261       "nothing on that basis is admitted by default"
    CANON-01-v2 L423            "SWTOR is excluded on the basis…"
    IMMEDIATE-ITEMS-BATCH L33   "Both carry the same core statement…"
    DECISION-RECORD-04 L34      "SWTOR is excluded on the basis…"
    DECISION-RECORD-04 L38      "Per-item approval is unchanged."

**`D-EXCEPT-07` narrows all seven. Before this sweep, one carried a pointer.**

**This is `§L75` at scale** — three restatements of one source hierarchy produced three different defects in a single session. **Here: seven restatements of one admission rule, one later ruling narrowing all of them, six silent.**

### And they are not identical, which is the part that matters

**`RULING-SWTOR-DEEP-HISTORY` says *never as a class*. `DECISION-RECORD-04` says *per-item approval is unchanged*. `CANON-01-v2` frames it as *a rejection of its events, not of the material as a source of ideas*.**

> **The third is the one `D-EXCEPT-07` is most compatible with — and it is the one nobody cites.** The Atlas's noun/verb split is exactly *"a rejection of its events, not of the material"*, and if that framing had been the cited one, `D-EXCEPT-07` would have read as an application rather than a breach.

**`D-EXCEPT-06` calls itself *"the first to breach `CANON-01`'s SWTOR exclusion."*** **On `CANON-01-v2`'s own wording it is not a breach at all.**

## 4 · The rule I would put in place of the one I named

> **The liability is not an absolute clause. It is a rule RESTATED across documents — because a later ruling narrows the rule and reaches only the copies someone remembers.**

**An absolute clause stated once is findable. The same clause stated seven times is seven places to miss.**

**`ATLAS-SEED-v3` already has the countermeasure — *"cite it; do not restate it. Restating is how v1 and v2 drifted apart"* — and this corpus predates my adopting it.** The seven exist because five documents each wanted to be self-contained, which is the same instinct that produced them and the reason they cannot be maintained together.
